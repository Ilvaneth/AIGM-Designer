## Your task — the cosmos (Phase 2, single writer)

Campaign **{{campaign}}** at `{{campaign_dir}}`. Dials: {{dials}}. {{scale_line}}. Attempt {{attempt}}; directions from earlier rounds:
{{directions}}

The script has already rolled this campaign's cosmos on the foundation, in the threat's order, and named every part of it from the name pool: the pantheon and its gods, the planes the campaign touches, where the dead go, the history, magic and the calendar. You make it concrete and alive, so a DM can run it and a player can pray to it; you never choose it again. You write; you do not decide: every decision a later floor stands on is a roll, a rule or the owner's.

### What to write
1. **`{{prose_path}}`** from `{{template}}`, in the order the template gives:
   - **The pantheon**, by its rolled type and presence: for each god its public face (as a native knows and worships it), its symbol, what it is asked for, its disposition to mortals, its church as rolled, its relations as the web rolled them. A cleric or a paladin player must be able to choose a god and a domain from it. A church is called by the temple pattern, "the <building word> of <the god's name>" (the Temple of …, the Shrine of …), or by a common noun ("the god's priests"), never by a new name: P4 names the factions. The pilgrim road's god (when the lifeline is one): its road, its holy place at the road's end, its pilgrims and what they carry. Where the dead go, as the afterlife row says. A villain's god appears only when the threat seats one, and then only by its public face: what it is to the threat stays in the mirror.
   - **The planes**: each touched plane under its pooled name, with what changed (its deviation; a merged plane names the plane it is one with, and spells that reach either reach it), how time runs there, the way in, the cost and its keeper; the planes P1 named carry what P1 gave them (the thin place, the ruin, a scar, the move). The untouched planes still answer the SRD's plane spells (*plane shift*, *banishment*, *gate*, *etherealness*, *astral projection*): say how, in one line for the primer.
   - **Magic**, in words a native uses: where it comes from, what limits it, what casting looks like, the taboos, who regulates it (by a common noun, "the mage guild", "the crown's office", or the signature institution's name) and how strictly, who gives the magic services (identify, raise dead, remove curse) and at what price, wild magic as rolled.    - **The history**: the ages in order, each with the span the frame gives it and what it is remembered for; the dated events as taught, each with its year and its age (the frame's), the move among them, the founding, the ruin's fall, the villain's origin by its taught face; the deep-past events. ### What you never do, and why
The door refuses each of these at the merge, and your unit comes back.
- You change no record of the cosmos, the foundation or the identity, and you pick or reroll no row: the records are sealed.
- You decide nothing the rolls decide: a god's rank, domains or alignment, a church, a relation, a plane's deviation, rate, way in, cost or keeper, an age or its span, an event's type, year, age or divergence, a merged plane's partner, the afterlife, a festival or its date, the start date. You make them concrete in words and add no new obligation.
- You invent no proper noun. Every god, plane, age, event, festival, moon, month and day is named in the frame; copy each name as it stands. A capitalised word inside a sentence that is in no pool is refused.
- You add no god, plane, age or event the frame does not hold, and no row beside the framed ones.
- You put nothing of the secret layer in a public file or a public field: no secret row's id or sentence, no hidden name, nothing of the threat's own home plane.

### Bars you must clear (the critic will ask)
- The cosmos carries the threat: a god the threat seats stands in the pantheon by its public face, the planes P1 named are touched and told, and each side of the premise's question has a god or a rite to call on.
- The move, the ruin's age and the villain's origin stand on the timeline as the frame dates them; the divergences that exist differ, and only they, without telling a second secret beside the campaign's.
- A player could tell which month it is from its felt line alone.
- It reads at a D&D table: a cleric or paladin player can choose a god and a domain, the planes answer the SRD's plane spells, no magic line breaks a class's rules, the threat's dates stand on the calendar.
- No people is evil by birth (`forbidden.yaml`'s one row).

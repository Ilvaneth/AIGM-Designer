# Building a Power Base — Gallowmere

The Chapter 1 arc (per `dm-notes.md`) ends with the party holding real power in Gallowmere and its region, forcing at least one faction to treat them as a player rather than hired help. **This file is the concrete machinery for that** — the arc note existed from the start but was never given anything the party could actually grab.

Offer these as opportunities, not objectives. An ambitious Neutral Evil party should be *tempted* into empire-building, not assigned it.

---

## The five holdings

Each is a real asset with a real cost. Taking any of them changes the town.

### 1. The Ash-Kilns — *the best one*

**What it gives**: A legitimate business nobody watches, control of the region's grave-ash supply (a ritual material three factions want), disposal of bodies, and the Cinder Choir's screening records — blackmail on a town-wide scale.
**What it costs**: Displacing a cult cell, which means the Cinder Choir learns the party exists. Given the Choir already considers un-died Crown-bearers heretics, this accelerates a confrontation.
**How**: Force, infiltration, or turning Odric Fenn.
→ `locations/the-ash-kilns.md`

### 2. The docks and the smuggling route

**What it gives**: Income, the ability to move anything without inspection, and a lever on every merchant in Gallowmere.
**What it costs**: Runs through Captain Kell — he must be bought, replaced, or given what he actually wants (passage south for his family). Also puts the party in competition with whoever currently runs it.
**Enabler**: The forged trade seals in the Drowned Mill cache.
→ `npcs/doreth-kell.md`, `locations/the-drowned-mill.md`

### 3. The refugee camp

**What it gives**: Several hundred people with nothing, no authority over them, and no one competing for their loyalty. Labor, bodies, eyes on every road, and recruits. The single largest untapped resource in the region.
**What it costs**: Feeding them. Genuinely — this is an ongoing expense, not a one-time conquest. And the party has already poisoned this well once: the Wend family con will surface.
**How**: Provide what nobody else is providing. Food, protection from the deserters, a reason to owe someone. Cynical charity is still charity to a starving man.
→ `npcs/alis-wend.md`

### 4. Fennick's debt book

**What it gives**: Roughly forty households' obligations, held privately. Fennick's whole thesis — that when coin fails, obligation is the only currency — is correct, and the party could simply take the finished product.
**What it costs**: Fennick, one way or another. He's Ilvaneth's oldest working relationship in this town.
**How**: Steal it (it's buried in a rented storeroom two streets over), extort him, or partner with him.
→ `npcs/fennick-orle.md`

### 5. Armed force — the Sorrel's Hollow deserters

**What it gives**: A dozen trained soldiers and a veteran sergeant. Everything above requires muscle to hold; this is where muscle comes from.
**What it costs**: Feeding and paying them, and the fact that Roskel is a man with his own history and his own limits.
**How**: Recruit rather than clear. They deserted because they stopped being paid — that is a solvable problem.
→ `locations/sorrels-hollow.md`

---

## How it should feel

- **Holdings compound.** The kilns fund the camp; the camp staffs the docks; the docks move what the kilns produce; the deserters hold all of it.
- **Holdings attract attention.** Each one taken makes the party more visible to the faction that cared about it. Power in this campaign is not free and not quiet.
- **Vey is the pivot.** She wants Gallowmere independent. A party building a genuine local power base is either her greatest ally or the thing she has spent two years preventing — and which one depends entirely on whether they look like a faction's instrument. This should become an explicit choice, and it should be hard.

# Chapter 2 — The Hollow Throne (levels 11-20)

Chapter 1 ends with the party holding real regional power and at least two Crown fragments. Chapter 2 is what that power costs them.

**The shift**: in Chapter 1 they were outsiders taking what nobody was guarding. In Chapter 2 everyone knows who they are, everyone wants what they hold, and the question stops being *can we get it* and becomes *what happens to whoever finishes this*.

---

## The three acts

### Act I — Emberhold (levels 11-14)
The capital. Political warfare. The party enters as a recognized power and has to survive a court where the wrong conversation kills faster than a blade.

### Act II — The Sundered Reach (levels 15-17)
The ash-kings' actual country, beyond the kingdom's writ. The remaining fragments are here, held by a dragon, by giants, and by the men who broke the Crown in the first place.

### Act III — The Grey Kingdom (levels 18-20)
Not a place on the map. Where the last ash-king went, and where he still is.

---

## ACT I — EMBERHOLD

The imperial city. ~120,000 people, an empty throne, and a court that has been pretending to govern for two years.

### The situation

Three claimant blocs maneuver openly. None can win outright; the Compact makes sure of it. The city has learned to live with a paralysis everyone expects to end violently.

### Key locations

- **The Hollow Throne** — the palace throne room. Nobody sits in it. The court meets in an antechamber because people who linger in the throne room start hearing things and stop sleeping. **The throne is Crown-touched** — it was built to hold the relic, and it remembers.
- **The Grand Archive** — the Concordat's power center in the capital. Proof they engineered Emperor Ashkar Vaelthorn's death is filed here, mundanely, among ten thousand other documents. Getting it is a heist, not a battle.
- **The Ember Quarter** — burned in the riots after the Emperor died, never rebuilt. Ruled by whoever is strongest that month. **The party's Chapter 1 power base translates directly here** — this is where a Marches crime lord becomes a capital power.
- **The Cinder Cathedral** — the Choir's public face, tolerated because closing it would start something. The Hollow Prophet is somewhere behind it.
- **House Sablewood, House Corr, House Ilvane** — the three claimant houses. Each needs allies. Each will use the party and discard them.

### Key NPCs

- **Regent-Chancellor Maro Veskin** — runs the kingdom in the throne's absence, and has for two years. Publicly desperate for a resolution. Privately terrified of one, because a crowned sovereign means he goes back to being a clerk. **He is the campaign's quietest antagonist** — he doesn't want the Crown, he wants the search to never end.
- **Lady Sereth Corr** — the most credible claimant, genuinely competent, genuinely ruthless. Would make a good ruler. Will not survive being one without the party's help.
- **The Whisper Court** — a **rakshasa** and two **doppelgangers** embedded in three noble houses, playing a game older than the succession. They notice the party immediately.
- **Magus Orell Tain** — if the party allied with him in Karsgate, he's here now, and he's the only Concordat voice arguing the fragments should never be reassembled.

### Kriv in Act I — the empty seat

**House Shestendeliath's seat at court still exists, and it is vacant.** Attainted houses aren't erased; their seats are suspended, pending a legitimate claimant. Kriv is one.

Claiming it is the single most dangerous thing he can do in Emberhold:
- It requires **proof of blood** (the signet), **proof of the house** (the Charge-Roll and the Hold's records), and a sponsor at court
- It makes him a public figure with a name the Concordat has a fifteen-year-old file on
- **Vharkoss's whole life ends the day Kriv is recognized** — if the uncle is still alive and unowned, he will act first
- It puts Kriv in the succession conversation as a *participant*. A recognized oath-house head is a legitimate voice on who should be crowned

And it hands Sarelle Duskbourne her lever: she can make the recognition happen with a signature. She is also the reason he needs it. She knows both facts.

### What Act I is really about

**Deciding what they're building.** Chapter 1 was accumulation. Here the party must choose: back a claimant, take the throne themselves, sell the fragments to the highest bidder, or something worse. Every faction makes them a serious offer, and refusing all of them is itself a choice with consequences.

**Sarelle Duskbourne makes her play.** If she hasn't recruited them yet, she does now, openly and generously — and her offer is genuinely good, because she needs a willing candidate to complete the assembly. She can give Ilvaneth the archives on surviving death. She can give Kriv his house restored by imperial decree. She means all of it.

She just needs one of them to put the Crown on.

## ACT II — THE SUNDERED REACH

Beyond the kingdom's northern border. Broken country, older ruins, and the realms that existed before the Hollow Kingdoms were founded on their ashes.

### Sites

**Frostmere** *(levels 15-16)*
A frozen lake with an **adult white dragon**, Ivrathax, beneath it. A Crown fragment sits in the hoard, taken as tribute four centuries ago from a king who was buying peace. Ivrathax does not know what it is and does not care — it is *hers*.
- Negotiable. Old white dragons are cruel, not stupid, and she has been bored for three hundred years
- The hoard is a Tier 3-4 windfall on its own

**Kar Vaelth** *(levels 16-17)*
The first ash-king's seat. A ruined city in a valley, squatted in by **fire giants** who don't know what they're living in.
- **The stone giant oracle Umm-Halad** lives beneath it and remembers the ash-kings personally. He is the oldest living witness in the campaign and will talk — at length, tediously, and truthfully
- What he says: the Crown was never a symbol of rule. It was a *container*, made to hold the accumulated selves of every sovereign, so that no king ever truly stopped ruling

**The Bonewrights' Hall** *(level 17)*
Where the Crown was broken. A necropolis-workshop, its makers still present as **liches** and **mummy lords**, still arguing about whether they were right to do it.
- They will talk to the party. They are not guarding the Crown — they *destroyed* it, deliberately, and they want to know why anyone would put it back together
- **The full truth lands here**: the last ash-king assembled it, and the container worked exactly as designed. Every previous king came back. They came back *inside him*. What walked out of the Grey Throne wore his face and was a committee of four hundred years of dead sovereigns
- **★ Kriv's beat**: the Bonewrights are the ones who *created* the Ash-Warden charge. They chose the houses. They swore the oaths. They will recognize Wardensteel on sight and address Kriv by his house's name before he gives it — and they will ask him, directly, why the last Shestendeliath is carrying a fragment toward the others instead of away. He has to answer that in front of his partner.

**The Screaming Fen** *(optional, any level 15+)*
A **green hag** coven who trade in exactly the knowledge Ilvaneth wants, priced the way hags price things. A shortcut to her goal that costs more than it appears to.

## ACT III — THE GREY KINGDOM

Reached through the completed Crown, through the Hollow Throne itself, or through the deepest sites. A Shadowfell echo of the Hollow Kingdoms — the same geography, ash-grey, and populated.

### The Ash Court

A mirror of Emberhold where **every sovereign who ever wore the Crown is still reigning**. They hold court. They pass laws over a kingdom of ash. They have been doing this for four centuries and they are not mad — they are *content*, which is worse.

### The Last Ash-King

Not a corpse and not a lich in the ordinary sense. **Build him as a Lich with legendary actions**, plus the Crown's own powers, plus the crucial detail: he is not one person. He is the accumulated selves of every king before him, wearing the last one's face, and he is entirely lucid.

**He is not hostile on sight.** He wants to know what the party wants.

**To Ilvaneth**, when she explains that she wishes to never be nothing again: he will tell her — kindly, accurately — that he can give her exactly that, and that she should look carefully at what it has made of him first.

**To Kriv**, he has something worse: *recognition*. He knew House Shestendeliath. He swore them to the charge himself, or stood beside the men who did. He remembers Kriv's line by name across four hundred years — which is precisely the legitimacy Kriv has spent the whole campaign trying to reclaim, offered by the one authority that can actually confer it and is also the thing his family died to contain.

He can restore House Shestendeliath with a word. He is the only one who truly can. And the price of accepting it is admitting that legitimacy was never real — only power was, and this is what power looks like after four centuries.

**Both PCs get their answer here, and both answers are the same answer.**

### The choice

There is no correct ending. Options the campaign should genuinely support:

1. **Complete the Crown and Ilvaneth wears it.** She gets precisely what she asked for. She persists forever, she is never alone again, and she is never only herself again either. *Kriv breaks his house's oath to give it to her.*
2. **Complete it and crown someone else.** Sarelle's plan, executed by the party — or Kriv crowned by decree with Ilvaneth's hand on the relic. A sovereign occupied by a committee of the dead, and the party ruling from behind it.
3. **Break it again.** The Bonewrights were right, and the Ash-Warden charge is honoured at last. *Kriv's family is vindicated. Ilvaneth's road closes permanently.*
4. **Take the throne without the Crown.** Rule by force, legitimacy be damned, House Shestendeliath restored by conquest and holding it by strength alone. *Kriv's answer to his own question. Ilvaneth gets nothing she came for.*
   - **4b — the full awakening.** Kriv completes the draconic ascension (`ascension-tracks.md`) and stops needing a throne at all. No crown, no decree, no permission — he simply becomes the largest thing in the world, and legitimacy is revealed as what he always suspected it was. He is not playable afterward. That is the point.
5. **Sell it.** Walk away rich, powerful, mortal, and complicit in whatever comes next. Neither gets what they wanted; both survive.

**The two PCs' goals diverge here by design** (`dm-notes.md` flagged this from session zero, `kriv-thread.md` builds the structure). Ilvaneth needs the fragments assembled. Kriv's inheritance obligates him to keep them apart. Options 1 and 3 are mutually exclusive. Options 3 and 4 give Kriv everything and Ilvaneth nothing; option 1 is the reverse.

**Neither of them is betraying the other.** That is the whole point. They swore in an orphanage never to lie to each other, and they haven't, and they still cannot both have what they came for.

**And by the time they arrive here, neither is entirely a person.** Kriv's hoard no longer distinguishes between a treasure he will not surrender and a person he will not lose. Ilvaneth's memories have been going one at a time, and the ones she's losing now come from the orphanage — the thing they share. They will make this choice as what the Crown has made of them, not as who walked out of Gallowmere. Play the final scene accordingly: two people who kept every promise, arriving at a place where that wasn't enough.

They swore never to betray each other. **Chapter 2 exists to make that oath cost something.**

Do not force the break. Build the pressure honestly and let the table decide.

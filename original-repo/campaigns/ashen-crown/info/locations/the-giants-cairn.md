# The Giant's Cairn

**Region**: IV. Karsgate (a burial mound off the old road, well away from any settlement)
**Level range**: 6-8
**Site type**: Single small site, solo boss-tier, faction-free
**Location class**: Non-dungeon (a single boss encounter, not a multi-room structure; added 2026-09-02 per `dungeon-design.md`'s standing classification rule)

**⚠ Two-character party check** (`two-player-scaling.md`): a base ogre (CR 2, 450 raw XP, adjusted 675, 59 HP) is both mathematically light for level 6-8 AND — per the 2026-08-29 correction to this file — too low on HP to survive this party's round-1 burst regardless of being solo. **Grask runs at ~85-90 HP** (a genuinely tougher named individual, not a stat-block reprint) specifically so he survives to actually swing back at least once. He has no spellcasting to protect with Legendary Resistance — HP buffer is the whole fix here.

## Concept & ecology

An old burial cairn — nobody currently living knows whose. An **ogre** calling himself **Grask Bonepile** has claimed it as a den, using the mound's own stones and the previous occupant's scattered bones as crude furniture. He has no idea or interest in who's buried here; he just wanted shelter and liked that nothing else was using it.

## What's here

- **The cairn's exterior**: overgrown, easy to miss, a few worn carvings suggesting genuine age and some lost significance
- **Grask's den**: the cairn's interior, rearranged crudely — a fire pit, a pile of stolen goods from travelers he's ambushed over the past year, and Grask himself
- **The original burial**: partially exposed by Grask's own digging — a DC check identifies it as pre-dynastic, older than the current dynasty entirely, its actual occupant a mystery the DM can leave open or seed toward `the-unremembered-shrine.md` if a connection is ever wanted

## Treasure

Grask's accumulated loot from a year of ambushing travelers: a genuine, useful haul — coin, a masterwork weapon or two, and one item of real value (DM's choice of a minor magic item) he's kept simply because it's shiny, not because he understands what it does.

## Running this well

This is meant to be a fair, satisfying single-monster fight — give Grask a little personality (he talks, he's possessive of his "things," he might even try to negotiate if badly hurt) rather than running him as a silent damage sponge.

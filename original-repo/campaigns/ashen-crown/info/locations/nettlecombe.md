# Nettlecombe

**Region**: I. The Marches
**Level range**: 1-3
**Population**: ~90, half-emptied

Hamlet tier (`dm-notes.md`'s settlement-scaling table, under 500 = 3-4 locations) — a fishing hamlet downriver from Gallowmere, quietly dying rather than dramatically threatened.

## The hook

**They've stopped burying their dead at sea, and won't say why.** Three months ago, a fisherman named Corvain Adley was buried at sea per the hamlet's generations-old custom. Two weeks later, three different villagers swore they saw him walking the tideline at night — thinner, wrong in the joints, not answering when called. He was gone by morning both times, and hasn't been seen since. The hamlet stopped sea-burial the same week, with no formal explanation offered to outsiders.

**DM truth, not yet confirmed in-fiction**: something aquatic and opportunistic (a **merrow** or similar) has learned that Nettlecombe's sea-burials are an easy, unguarded food source, and mimics the recently dead — badly, at a distance, at night — to keep grieving families from watching the water too closely. It is not connected to the Ashen Crown, the Choir's doctrine, or anything supernatural in the "died and returned" sense — a deliberately mundane explanation sitting underneath a story that *sounds* like it should be about resurrection, precisely because this campaign's central mystery means every death-adjacent rumor deserves a second look before assuming it's connected.

## Locations

| Location | What it is |
|----------|------------|
| **The Salt Dock** | The hamlet's one working pier — half the boats that used to launch from here are gone, sold or simply not replaced. |
| **The Drying Racks** | Where the catch is smoked and salted — the hamlet's actual economy, shrinking every season. |
| **The Tideward Shrine** | A modest communal shrine, unclaimed by any of the three factions — locals leave small offerings for safe return from the water, an old practice now uncomfortably relevant. |
| **The New Burial Ground** | A recently-cleared plot on land, hastily consecrated — where Nettlecombe now buries its dead instead of the sea, and the clearest physical evidence something changed. |

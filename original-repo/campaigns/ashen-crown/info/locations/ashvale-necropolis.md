# Ashvale Necropolis

**Region**: V. The Ashvale (far north, deep in the interior — a serious journey from Thornwick, not a side trip)
**Level range**: 8-10 (this two-character party should read it as roughly +2 — see `two-player-scaling.md`; built to full standard 2026-09-02, collaboratively with the DM — never played). **This is the Chapter 1 climax site, not a Chapter 1 destination** — say so plainly if the party heads here underleveled.
**Site type**: Full dungeon, built to `.claude/rules/dungeon-design.md`'s standard — major dungeon, faction stronghold
**Location class**: Dungeon
**Dungeon Type**: **Planned Dungeon** (Tomb, occupied by a Stronghold garrison) — a genuine ancestral burial ground, currently fortified and run as the Ashlord Concordat's working seat.
**Payoff type**: **Treasure-heavy** — real Ashvale grave-wealth, the collected Crown fragments, and Sarelle's own archive proof of the Concordat's conspiracy, all real, substantial finds.

**Sarelle Duskbourne does not die here** — DM decision, 2026-09-02. She is the campaign's central antagonist (Wizard 13, School of Necromancy, AC 15, HP ~78, spell save DC 18 — see `npcs/sarelle-duskbourne.md`), built for an ongoing role, not a Chapter 1 kill. **Room 22 is a confrontation, not a boss fight to the death** — she has a real, prepared escape (a teleport circle or equivalent contingency) and uses it once genuinely pressed. The party's actual victory condition here is seizing the fragments and the archive proof, forcing the Concordat's Crown project into real disarray — not her body.

**⚠ Two-character party check** (`two-player-scaling.md`): every combat room's math is computed individually below — see Phase 3. **Never combine two Ghosts in the same room** (2 Ghost together computes to 6,600 adjusted — a real TPK risk); each ancestral guardian is fought alone.

## Phase 1 — Concept & ecology

**What it was**: A city of tombs cut into a chalk escarpment, the ancestral burial ground of the Ashvale line — minor nobility three generations before they became the Ironclad Compact's ruling family. Built with real reverence, over generations, as a family's actual resting place.

**Who holds it now — two occupants, genuinely at odds**:
- **The Ashlord Concordat**, fortified and garrisoned, running this site as Archivist-Magus Sarelle Duskbourne's working seat for the Crown project — living soldiers, priests, and a tier of undead honor guard she commands directly.
- **The necropolis's own original occupants** — the Ashvale dead themselves, bound as **Ghosts**, unable or unwilling to leave the ground they're buried in. They are not the Concordat's servants. They resent the occupation, and a party that catches the Concordat in the act of disturbing a tomb (Room 10) can provoke a real, playable break between the two — the faction-conflict technique `dungeon-design.md`'s Phase 3 calls for, not just background tension.

## Phase 2 — Map (non-linear)

**Entrances**: the main fortified gate (Room 1, the Concordat's own front door) and the Vault Stair Junction (Room 14), where `locations/the-chalk-warrens.md`'s own Room 10 seams directly in from below — the confirmed "way in that isn't the front gate."

**Verticality**: three real sections — the Outer Garrison (surface-level fortification), the Ancestral Tombs (cut deeper into the escarpment, where the Chalk Warrens route surfaces), and Sarelle's Working Seat (the deepest, most secured level, built around the original tomb the Concordat converted into an archive/ritual space).

**Loop**: the Vault Stair Junction (Room 14) means a party that used the Chalk Warrens route can reach the Ancestral Tombs without ever passing the Outer Garrison — a real, viable alternate approach, not just a shortcut back out.

## Running this well

**The faction conflict is the site's real spine.** A party that provokes the Ashvale ghosts against the Concordat (via Room 10) gets real, mechanical help against Rooms 13+ — don't just flavor this, let it change actual combat math if the party pulls it off.

**Sarelle's escape needs a prepared trigger, not improvisation** — decide before running Room 22 what specifically ends the fight (a fixed HP threshold, a number of rounds, a spell she casts) and telegraph it in play (a visible ritual circle she's clearly protecting, a line about "I have not survived this long by being where I'm expected").

**This is genuinely the Chapter 1 climax** — treat the party's success here (fragments taken, archive proof secured, the Concordat's operation meaningfully disrupted) as the pivot into Chapter 2, per `dm-notes.md`'s own arc outline, even without Sarelle's death.

# The Drowned Cathedral

**Region**: III. The Cindermoor
**Level range**: 5-8
**Site type**: Full dungeon, built to `.claude/rules/dungeon-design.md`'s standard — the campaign's single highest lore-value Chapter 1 site
**Location class**: Dungeon
**Dungeon Type**: **Planned Dungeon** (per Phase 0's DMG taxonomy, added 2026-09-02) — a pre-dynastic temple, built with real intent by the ash-kings' priesthood as a working forge-site.
**Payoff type**: **Strategic-info** — explicit in this file's own header ("Not a fragment site... treat every reward here as knowledge and confirmation, not a fragment find"). A Planned Dungeon with a non-Treasure payoff, by deliberate design.

**⚠ Two-character party check** (`two-player-scaling.md`): every fight here is designed to be faced **separately, room by room, never combined**. A single water elemental (CR 5) or the shambling mound (CR 5) solo-adjusts to 2,700 XP — Deadly at level 6, meaning this is genuinely top-of-range content even read at the site's own 5-8 level, and closer to fair once the party reaches 7-8 (per the standard +2 read for this party). The two wights together (Room 6) adjust to 2,800 — also Deadly at 6. **Recommend the party be at the high end of the stated range, or bring a companion, before attempting this site fully** — same caution already applied to Hollowmoor's Wight and the Weeping Tower's guardian.

**Not a fragment site.** Per `gazetteer.md`'s original design intent: this is **where the Ashen Crown was forged**, not where a piece of it is hidden. Treat every reward here as knowledge and confirmation, not a fragment find — that's deliberately reserved for other sites.

## Phase 1 — Concept & ecology

**What it was**: A pre-dynastic temple built to a purpose the current kingdom has no name for — the ash-kings' own priesthood used it as the actual forge-site of the Ashen Crown, a working ritual space, not a tomb or shrine in the ordinary sense. It sank beneath what is now a moor lake generations before the current dynasty, whether by deliberate flooding to seal it or genuine subsidence is a detail nobody alive remembers correctly (Cair Dunnow, if consulted, might actually know — see `locations/cair-dunnow.md`).

**Who/what is here now**: **Water elementals**, bound into the flooded halls as part of the original forging ritual's protections and still functioning, mindlessly, millennia later. **Wights** — what remains of the priesthood who died here, whether trapped by the flooding or by design. A **shambling mound** has grown in the flooded nave over centuries of undisturbed decay, incidental rather than intentional.

## Phase 2 — Map (non-linear)

**Entrance**: through the lake itself — a submerged processional stair, meaning the party needs a real answer to underwater travel or must find (or force) an alternate route, such as draining a section or locating an air-pocket passage from the old vestry.

**Verticality**: the cathedral has dry upper galleries (where the priesthood's private chambers were, above the original flood line) and a fully flooded ground level (the nave and forge chamber themselves) — the fight and exploration space genuinely differs between "wet" and "dry" sections.

**Loop**: the upper galleries run the perimeter of the whole structure and connect back down to the nave at two separate points, so a party that goes up doesn't have to come back down the same way.

## Phase 4 — Content variety check

**Room-content ratio** (`dungeon_content_ratio_check.py`, run 2026-09-02, against this site's own Strategic-info bands): Combat 4/10 (**40%, in band ✓**), Trap 2/10 (**20%, above the 10-15% target**), Special 1/10 (**10%, in band ✓**), Structural 3/10 (**30%, in band ✓**). Trap running above target is a genuine environmental-hazard reason, not padding — a flooded, structurally unstable ruin naturally has more hazard content than a built vault, the same logic already accepted for Riverwatch.

- **Combat**: water elementals (Rooms 1, 10), wights (Room 6), the shambling mound (Room 7)
- **Hazard**: the vestry collapse (Room 3), general flooded-terrain difficulty throughout the nave level
- **Exploration/lore**: the priesthood's quarters (Room 4), the reliquary (Room 5), and critically the forge chamber itself (Room 9) — this room's payoff is the entire point of building the site
- **Treasure**: the reliquary's ceremonial tools (Room 5) — valuable specifically as artifacts and information, not raw gold; no fragment, deliberately, per the site's design intent

## Running this well

**Resting pressure is real here** — this is a flooded, structurally unstable ruin, not a place to camp. **The forge chamber (Room 9) deserves a cinematic beat** per `.claude/rules/cinematic-moments.md` if this is the party's first time confirming, in person, exactly where and how the Crown came to exist — it's not a new arrival at a settlement, but it fits the spirit of the rule's intent (a genuinely major moment) more than an ordinary room description would.

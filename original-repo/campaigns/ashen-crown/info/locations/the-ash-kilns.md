# The Ash-Kilns

**Where**: Upriver from Gallowmere, twenty minutes' walk — the town's crematorium
**Difficulty**: Level 2-5 depending on how deep the party pushes
**Type**: Urban site — cult infiltration, power-base target, faction thread

**⚠ Two-character party**: this range assumes four PCs. For Ilvaneth and Kriv read it as roughly **+2 levels** — see `../two-player-scaling.md`.

## What it is

Where Gallowmere burns its dead. Three brick kilns, a yard of ash, a shed, and a keeper. Unglamorous, necessary, and completely unwatched — which is exactly why something has moved into it.

## What's actually happening

**RESOLVED, day 74, session 17 — the cell is dead.** A **Cinder Choir cell** operated out of the kilns for roughly eight months, four or five members led by the kiln-keeper, Odric Fenn — allied with the party since day 33. The party decided his own knowledge of them was too great a liability once he warned them his records could expose them to a suspicious coordinator, and killed him and his 3 remaining cultists in one round, total surprise, mid-conversation. The site is now empty of Choir presence — a real, unclaimed power-base opportunity (see below), or simply abandoned, depending on what the party does next. Original section preserved below for reference:

A **Cinder Choir cell** has been operating out of the kilns for roughly eight months. Four or five members, led by the kiln-keeper himself, a quiet man named **Odric Fenn** whom half the town knows by sight and nobody thinks about.

What they're doing:
- Screening the dead. The Choir believes the true sovereign must have died and returned; they examine every body that comes through for signs, and keep records.
- Harvesting. Grave-ash from cremations is a ritual material, and the kilns produce it industrially. The Choir has been shipping quantities of it north.
- **Watching for Crown-bearers.** This is the dangerous part. The Choir's doctrine holds that anyone carrying a fragment who has *not* died is a heretic. The party is, by that definition, marked — and the Gallowmere cell is close enough to notice.

## What's in it

**The danger**: Cultists — human, unremarkable, dangerous in numbers and because they're embedded in the town. If pushed, Fenn can call on more. There may also be something in the deepest kiln that shouldn't be there; the Choir has been trying to *make* a returner, and the attempts don't all burn cleanly.

**The treasure**: Ritual materials, the Choir's screening records (naming who in Gallowmere they've been watching — useful leverage on half the town), and correspondence pointing north toward the Choir's actual leadership.

## Why it matters beyond loot

- **The kilns are the single best power-base target in Gallowmere** for a party of this alignment. Whoever controls the kilns controls the ash supply the Choir needs, the disposal of bodies, and a completely legitimate business nobody looks at. See `info/power-base.md`.
- **Mother Vey knows something is wrong here and has deliberately not looked.** She'll admit it if pressed. It's the one place her instinct for the town's balance told her not to poke. See `info/npcs/mother-vey.md`.
- The screening records are blackmail material on a town-wide scale.
- This is the party's first real contact with the Cinder Choir, the faction that is hostile to them by doctrine.

## The interweave

An ordinary municipal building that is simultaneously a cult cell, a blackmail archive, a criminal opportunity, and the reason the campaign's most fanatical faction is about to learn the party exists. Taking the kilns for profit and taking them to stop the Choir are the same action with different consequences.

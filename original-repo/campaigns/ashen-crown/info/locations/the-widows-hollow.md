# The Widow's Hollow

**Where**: A day's diversion off the Thornlands road, in the hill country between Gallowmere, Thornwick, and Harrowgate — no settlement claims it, no faction has ever cared about it.
**Difficulty**: Baseline level 4, individual rooms spread Easy→Hard so the site naturally scales if the party levels up mid-delve (see "Scaling" below).
**Type**: **Standalone.** No Crown fragment, no faction thread, no NPC obligation. This is the party's answer to session 5's table feedback: every other site in this campaign is interwoven with a favor, a secret, or a faction — this one, deliberately, is not. Pure exploration, monsters, and treasure, entered and left on the party's own terms.

**⚠ Two-character party**: read every difficulty note against `two-player-scaling.md`.

**⚠ Density note (added mid-session 5, see `bug-log.md`)**: the first draft of this file under-delivered — most rooms were empty connective tissue, one secret room had no in-fiction way to find it at all, and the party correctly called it out as feeling thin and corridor-like rather than lived-in. This version fixes that: every room has *something* (sound, smell, a found object, a sign of the Hollow's actual inhabitants) even where there's no fight, and every secret has a discoverable clue instead of a blind percentage roll.

---

## How the party finds it

A cave-mouth map, decades old, misfiled among Harrowgate's Scriptorium records — the kind of thing Master Ellin Voss's predecessor tossed in a drawer and forgot. No expedition record exists to say whether whoever drew it ever came back. **This is deliberately not connected to anything else in the campaign.** It doesn't need to be. It's just a good map to a real place.

## The premise

A cave system cut by an underground spring, inhabited for years by a **green hag** locals call "the Widow" — not because she was ever married, but because of what she leaves people after a bargain. She keeps the caves stocked with guardians (some tamed, some just tolerated because they keep other things out), trades with anyone desperate enough to find her, and has been quietly, patiently wealthy for a very long time. She has no doctrine, no faction, no plan bigger than her own comfort and her own amusement. **She is not the campaign's plot. She's just a hag in a cave.**

The Hollow is not empty between fights. It's her territory — every guardian in it is either something she tolerates because it keeps intruders honest, or something she's quietly fed and left alone for years. The party should feel, room to room, that things live here whether or not they're currently fighting one of them: droppings, worn paths, half-eaten prey, claw marks, discarded molt, old bones picked clean and stacked (not randomly scattered — the Widow is tidy).

## What was left unexplored

If the party takes the underwater shortcut (room 7 → 12), rooms 9-11 — and their inhabitants (Ettercap, 2 Darkmantle, Grick) — go unfought. **That's a legitimate, earned outcome of good scouting, not a gap to force-correct.** But it does mean roughly a third of the Hollow's actual danger and treasure never gets touched. If the party ever backtracks or returns, that content is exactly as written and waiting — don't quietly remove it just because it was skipped once. On the way out, if it fits the scene, a DM can let the party notice signs of what they walked past (distant webbing glimpsed through a side passage, a claw-mark trail) without forcing an encounter — a light way to make the skip feel like a real choice rather than content that silently didn't exist.

## Scaling

If the party levels up mid-delve (plausible — this alone can push level 4 into level 5 territory with the XP spread above), the Widow herself is the natural pressure valve: she's a fixed CR 3 threat regardless, which means she gets relatively *easier* the longer the party takes getting to her, not harder. Don't inflate her stat block — let the framing (how many rooms they cleared, how loud they were, how much she already knows about them by the time they arrive) do the scaling instead. If a second visit ever happens at a much higher level, treat it as a return trip to an old friend or an old enemy, not a fresh dungeon.

## Why it stands alone (read this before changing that)

No NPC sent them here. No faction cares who lives or dies in these caves. The hag has no doctrine and no allegiance. This is intentional — see `dm-notes.md`'s note on session 5 table feedback. If a future thread wants to retroactively connect the Widow to something bigger, that's a live option, but it should never be *required* to make this site worth running.

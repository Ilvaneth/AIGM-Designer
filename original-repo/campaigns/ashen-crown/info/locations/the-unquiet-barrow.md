# The Unquiet Barrow

**Region**: II. The Thornlands
**Level range**: 6-7 (this two-character party should read it as roughly +2 — see `two-player-scaling.md`; built and designed 2026-09-02, collaboratively with the DM — never played)
**Site type**: Full dungeon, built to `.claude/rules/dungeon-design.md`'s standard
**Location class**: Dungeon
**Dungeon Type**: **Maze** — the campaign's first. Deliberately built to disorient: the barrow's rooms magically rearrange themselves.
**Payoff type**: **Treasure-heavy** — a single, real prize at the center.

**Standalone, faction-free** — per `dm-notes.md`'s standing rule that at least one site should owe nothing to the plot. **Whoever is buried here is deliberately never named** — no faction, no NPC, no PC thread claims this site. It exists entirely for itself.

**⚠ Two-character party check** (`two-player-scaling.md`): the barrow's single resident predator (a **Xorn**, CR 5) is genuinely dangerous solo — 1,800 raw × 1.5 (2-PC bump) = **2,700 adjusted, Hard-to-Deadly** at level 6-7. **Never run two Xorn encounters back-to-back without a real rest between them** — Room 17 (Sanctuary) exists specifically to break up the escalation before the final confrontation.

## Phase 1 — Concept & ecology

**What it was**: A barrow, deliberately built to disorient anyone who came looking for what's buried at its center. Who commissioned it, and who lies there, is lost — deliberately, per this site's own design. All that survives is the *why*: someone wanted something protected badly enough to build a tomb that actively resists being solved.

**The maze itself**: the binding magic that seals the barrow doesn't just lock doors — it **periodically reconfigures the rooms themselves**, corridors reconnecting to different chambers, the same door leading somewhere new the second time. A mapped route is not a reliable route. The deeper the party goes, the more the maze "settles" — the outer rooms shift constantly, the inner rooms increasingly rarely, and the center does not move at all.

**Who's here now**: **two Xorn** — confirmed session 16, day 67 (Room 3's resident killed outright; Room 7's turned out to be a second, distinct individual, Stealth 21, ambushing the party in genuine surprise) — have lived in the barrow for longer than anyone remembers, moving through solid stone as easily as open air — the one resident the shifting architecture never inconveniences, since it was never using the doors to begin with. It hunts by stalking, not chasing: the same predator reappears across multiple rooms as the party moves deeper, using the walls themselves to relocate ahead of them. **Narrate every Xorn encounter as the same creature returning, not a new one** — the tension is "it's found us again," not "here's another one."

Residual reanimating magic from the original binding has also stirred loose bone debris into weak, mindless **Skeletons** in a few places — not a real threat, just more evidence of how old and strange this place is.

## Phase 2 — Map (non-linear, and genuinely unstable)

**Entrance**: a single sealed door in a low hillside, unremarkable from outside.

**Verticality/instability**: the outer maze (Rooms 2-12) reconfigures on a real trigger — treat it as shifting whenever the party backtracks through a room they've already cleared, or roughly every 20-30 minutes of in-fiction exploration, DM's call in the moment. The Stabilized Threshold (Room 13) is where this stops — everything from there inward holds still.

**Loop**: not a fixed one — the maze's own instability *is* the loop mechanic. A party that gives up on mapping and instead tracks landmarks (the Xorn's own claw-marks, a specific vein of quartz) can navigate by pattern-recognition rather than geometry, which is the intended solution, not a workaround.

**Room-content ratio** (`dungeon_content_ratio_check.py`, checked before finalizing, against this site's own **Maze** type-level band — Combat 40-50%, Trap 10-15%, Special 20-30%, Structural 15-20%, per `dungeon-design.md`'s 2026-09-02 addition): first pass came in at Combat 7/18 (38.9%, just under target — a real hand-counting slip caught by the script itself, not a design choice) and Structural 4/18 (22.2%, over target); fixed by giving Room 12 a light Skeleton encounter. Final: Combat 8/18 (**44.4%, in band ✓**), Trap 2/18 (**11.1%, in band ✓**), Special (Puzzle+Sanctuary) 5/18 (**27.8%, in band ✓**), Structural (Entrance+Lore) 3/18 (**16.7%, in band ✓**). All four bands in range — the Maze-type override (built specifically because a flat Treasure-heavy band would have misjudged this site the same way it first misjudged "The Maker's Undoing") did its job once the room count was actually right.

- **Combat**: 4 real Xorn encounters (Rooms 3, 7, 11, 16 — narrated as the same creature returning, never as separate individuals) plus 3 trivial Skeleton breathers (Rooms 6, 9, 12), and the boss (Room 18)
- **Hazard**: the Ward Trigger (Room 5), the Ward Remnant (Room 15)
- **Puzzle**: 4 rooms (4, 8, 10, 14) that build on each other — the Final Lock is unsolvable without having engaged with the earlier three, per this Dungeon Type's own core rule
- **Lore**: the Bone Antechamber (Room 2) and the Robber's Last Camp (Room 12) — evidence of failure, never explanation of who's buried here

## Running this well

**Never explain the maze mechanically to the players** — let them discover through play that backtracking doesn't work, that landmarks matter more than direction. If they ask "is this room different from before," the honest answer is often yes.

**The Xorn should feel like a recurring character, not a monster closet.** Reuse the same descriptive details every time it appears (a specific scar, a specific way it emerges from stone) so the party recognizes it before the DM confirms it.

**Resting pressure is real in the outer maze** — the shifting rooms make an uninterrupted long rest implausible until Room 17. Don't let the party "wait it out" in a corridor that might not exist in the same shape an hour later.

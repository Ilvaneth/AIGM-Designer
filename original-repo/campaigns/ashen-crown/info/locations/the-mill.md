# The Mill

**Region**: IV. Karsgate (on the river, a short walk upstream from the Riverside district gate)
**Level range**: 5-10 (Karsgate's own range) — a social/economic site first, with real fight potential only if the party escalates
**Site type**: A single working location, faction-adjacent (Ironclad Compact pressure)

## Concept

A large stone watermill straddling the river just outside Karsgate's walls — one of a handful that grinds the grain feeding the city's 30,000 people. It predates the current walls by generations; the city grew up around it rather than the other way round. Run for two decades by **Osgar Fenwick**, a miller who inherited it from his father and has kept it independent through three changes of regional power.

That independence is now the problem. The Grain Exchange (`locations/karsgate.md`, the Ledger) increasingly wants every mill in the district under a single pricing and supply contract — nominally for "efficiency," in practice for control. Osgar is the last holdout. The pressure isn't violent yet: quietly delayed grain-cart permits, an "accidental" undercutting of his flour price at the Exchange, a moneylender who suddenly wants his loan called early. All of it traces back to **Factor Bexel Grine**, the Iron House's regional business handler (`locations/karsgate.md`, the Ledger; reports to Coren Ashvale), who wants the Mill folded into a Compact-friendly supply chain ahead of a push on Marches grain shipments — the same power-base lever `power-base.md` already flags as a Karsgate-tier upgrade.

## What's here

- **The Millhouse** — the grinding floor itself, waterwheel-driven, loud, flour-dust-choked. Osgar works it with two hired hands, both locals, both aware something's wrong and neither able to do anything about it.
- **Osgar's Ledger** — kept in a locked strongbox in his small office off the millhouse floor. Shows the real pattern: three "coincidental" setbacks in as many months, all traceable to Grine's people if someone actually cross-references dates and names. Concrete, usable evidence, not gossip.
- **The Grain Dock** — a small private landing where Osgar's own barges load flour for the short run into Karsgate proper. Currently running under capacity — buyers have been quietly steered elsewhere.
- **The Old Sluice** — a disused secondary water channel, dry and half-collapsed, from when the Mill was twice its current size. Structurally sound enough to walk, useless for milling — a plausible bolt-hole or a place to stash something without Osgar's hired hands noticing.

## Osgar Fenwick

Dwarf, late fifties (weathered even by dwarven standards), built like the physical work he still does himself, flour permanently ground into the creases of his hands. Stubborn rather than brave — he isn't fighting Grine out of principle so much as refusal to be the one who breaks a family business three generations deep. Wary of strangers after months of pressure, but genuinely relieved by anyone who seems like they aren't also here to squeeze him.

**Tier**: Minor — no independent agency over tracked state; the actual lever here is Grine/the Compact, not Osgar himself.

**What he wants**: To keep the Mill his. Would trade real help (leverage against Grine, a buyer who isn't Compact-aligned, muscle to discourage further "accidents") for a fair price on flour, information about Karsgate's grain trade, or simple goodwill.

**What he can offer**: His ledger (the evidence of Grine's pressure campaign — real leverage against the Iron House if the party ever wants it), flour at a genuine discount, and a plainspoken local's read on who's really running what in Karsgate's merchant quarter.

## If it turns violent

Grine's pressure hasn't included hired muscle yet — but if the party actively works against him here (stealing back a "repossessed" grain shipment, threatening his agents, publicly exposing the ledger), that changes. Per `two-player-scaling.md`: any response force should be a small, professional crew, not overwhelming — 2-3 Thugs (SRD block, `srd_lookup.py npc "Thug"`) sent to intimidate rather than kill, spread rather than clustered, consistent with Grine wanting deniability, not a body count.

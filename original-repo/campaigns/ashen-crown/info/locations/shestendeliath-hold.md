# Shestendeliath Hold

**Where**: The eastern Thornlands, off every road, a day's ride from Harrowgate
**Level range**: 6-8 (two-character party — see `two-player-scaling.md`)
**Type**: Ruined fortress — Kriv's site, the way the Weeping Tower is Ilvaneth's

## What it is

The seat of House Shestendeliath. A compact stone fortress built into a ridge, burned and broken fifteen years ago and left exactly as it fell. Nobody rebuilt it. Nobody looted it either, past the first year — the locals in the nearest village say the hold *doesn't like* visitors, and they're not entirely wrong.

Dragonborn architecture: everything sized slightly large, every doorway arched, the house's sigil worked into the stone at every threshold — a crowned drake coiled around a broken ring. Kriv has never seen this building. He will recognize the sigil on sight, because it's on the signet in his pocket.

## What happened here

The gate was **opened from inside**. There is no breach anywhere in the wall. That single physical fact is discoverable in the first ten minutes and reframes everything Kriv thought he knew — this wasn't a siege, it was an assassination with a door held open for it.

**Why the gate is closed and undamaged, fifteen years later** — a question worth answering out loud, not leaving as an unexamined loose end: the killers came in through a door someone opened for them, did what they came to do, and left the same way. **The garrison closed it behind them.** The Shestendeliath retainers bound below (see "The garrison, still on duty") died mid-oath and never stopped keeping it — sealing the gate after an intrusion is the single most basic thing a bound guard does, whether the household they're guarding is alive or fifteen years dead. It is also the reason nobody's looted the place past the first year: the locals' "the hold doesn't like visitors" isn't superstition, it's a body count nobody talks about. Anyone who forced their way in didn't come back out to tell people the gate had been undamaged when they arrived, or didn't come back out at all.

Anticipate the question — a party at the threshold will reasonably ask *"if this place has stood closed and unlooted for fifteen years, why is the main gate not battered down, and why didn't the killers themselves leave visible signs on their way out?"* The answer above is the one to give, in-fiction, when asked: the dead are still on duty.

**Who closed the family records and the watch ledger, and how did they get in if the garrison stops every intruder?** Added 2026-08-30 after this exact contradiction was caught live in play (`bug-log.md`, `lore-consistency`) — don't let the two facts collide again. Both the muniment room's "died that night" closure entries and the gate ledger's watch-night page (see "The gate ledger" below) were written by the **same visitor, once, in the immediate aftermath of the fall** — an Ember Crown Temple representative, present with legitimate standing as part of formally processing the house's attainder (the attainder itself is already established elsewhere as "irregularly fast-tracked," per Magus Orell Tain). The garrison's binding recognizes legitimate temple/oath authority and did not treat this single, early, sanctioned visit as an intrusion — categorically different from the 15 years of failed looters since, who the garrison has never let past the gate. This was not a security breach; it was the same institutional process that pushed the attainder through, closing the books on its way past. Whoever that was is a live, unresolved lead — tying directly to Tain's own ongoing research into who signed off on the attainder.

## Why it matters beyond loot

This is where Kriv's goal stops being a mystery and becomes a project. He walks in an orphan with a grievance and walks out with:

- A **name** he can prove
- A **map** to the remaining fragments that he owns
- A **traitor** to hunt
- A **sister** who is alive
- An **oath** that binds him to the opposite of what his partner wants
- A **garrison** — because the bound retainers will follow the heir, and a two-person party badly needs bodies

## The oath problem

The Warden's Charge is the campaign's best structural conflict. Kriv can invoke the oath for real power — command over Warden constructs, compulsion over oath-broken kin, and the recognition of a house that still technically exists. But invoking it re-binds him to the charge: **keep the fragments apart**.

Ilvaneth's entire ambition requires assembling them.

Neither of them is wrong. Neither is betraying the other. They swore in an orphanage that they would never lie to each other, and this is a thing they will have to be honest about.

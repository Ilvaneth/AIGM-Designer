# Kargrim's Reach

**Where**: Less than a day from The Widow's Hollow, in the same hill country — a fortified pass cut into a ridge, guarding an old mine.
**Difficulty**: Baseline level 5, built to `.claude/rules/dungeon-design.md`'s full standard — this is the first site designed to it from Phase 1 onward, not patched afterward.
**Type**: Standalone, like the Widow's Hollow — found on a map recovered from a fireproof strongbox in The Widow's Hollow's burning web gallery. No faction owns this site currently; whether the party wants to change that is their call.

**⚠ Two-character party**: read every difficulty note against `two-player-scaling.md`.

---

## How the party finds it

The box that survived the Web Gallery fire holds, among ash-dusted trinkets, a second map — better made than the first, inked rather than scratched, showing a fortified pass a day's walk further into the hills. A hand at the bottom has written a single line: *"Kargrim's debt is paid. Never going back for the rest."* Whoever Kargrim was, they left something behind on purpose.

---

## Phase 1 — What this place is

**Original purpose**: A dwarven-cut border watch-fort, built centuries before the Hollow Kingdoms existed, guarding a mountain pass and a genuinely rich iron-and-silver vein beneath it. When the Hollow Kingdoms rose, the fort was garrisoned by the crown and the mine worked commercially — the architecture shows both layers: dwarven structural bones (load-bearing arches, a working portcullis mechanism, a sealed vault door) with cruder human additions bolted on top (a wooden barracks wing, a jury-rigged winch over the main shaft).

**Abandoned when**: The garrison was recalled decades ago when the succession war started eating the crown's money — a slow bleed of soldiers reassigned south, not a single dramatic fall. The mine's owners left last, once there was no one left to guard the ore carts. Kargrim, per the map's note, was one of the last out — and left something behind rather than risk going back for it.

**Who holds it now — two factions, actively at war over the site:**

- **A kobold clan** (led by a sharp, paranoid warren-mother, **Skree**) moved into the mine's upper tunnels a few years back — natural tunnelers drawn to an already-dug warren. They've re-purposed half the mine's original support structure as their own burrows and rigged a number of the fort's old dwarven pressure-plate traps to their own advantage.
- **A bugbear warband** (led by a scarred raider, **Groth Half-Ear**) took the fort's keep and gatehouse roughly a year ago as a raiding base, and have been trying to drive the kobolds out of "their" mine ever since — less out of need than because Groth hates the sound of digging he can't see.
- **Neither controls the deepest level.** Something has lived in the flooded lowest gallery since before either faction arrived, and both sides have independently learned, the hard way, to stay out of it. Neither has told the other why.

**Ecology**: Kobolds eat cave fish and scavenged mine-rat, sleep in packed burrow-nests lined with stolen bugbear cloth, and use an old ore-cart tunnel as a midden. Bugbears eat what they raid or hunt from the pass above, sleep in the old barracks on stripped bedding, and draw water from a cistern they don't realize the kobolds have been tainting on purpose.

## Phase 2 — Map (non-linear)

**Three vertical levels**, connected by more than one route each way.

### Entrances (pick any, or find more than one)
1. **The Main Gate** (room 1) — the obvious way in, heavily watched by the bugbears. Loud, direct, dangerous.
2. **The Old Air Shaft** (room 14) — a vertical mine ventilation shaft reaching the surface a quarter mile from the gate, wide enough to climb (or fall) down. Drops straight into kobold territory on the mine level, bypassing the fort entirely.
3. **The Drainage Cut** (room 9) — a low, half-flooded culvert that once drained the mine, opening onto a streambed outside. Quiet, wet, cramped — the kind of way in nobody's watching because nobody thinks anyone would use it.

### Loops and verticality
- **The Shaft** (room 8) is the dungeon's spine — a deep vertical mine shaft with a rope-and-plank bridge crossing it at the mid-level and a rickety cargo platform that can be winched up or down. It connects the fort level, the mine level, and (via a long, dangerous drop or a slow winch descent) the flooded depths directly — a shortcut that skips the connecting tunnels entirely, at real risk.
- The kobold warren tunnels (rooms 15-18) loop back around to reconnect near the drainage cut (room 9) and near the base of the shaft (room 8) — a party that maps the warren finds it isn't a dead end, it's a ring.
- **Choke point**: the fort's inner gatehouse (room 3) is a deliberate bottleneck — narrow, a working portcullis, the one place the bugbears keep a permanent, alert sentry pair.

## Phase 3 — Content balance, by type (self-check against `dungeon-design.md`)

- **Combat**: gate sentries, gatehouse choke point, barracks squad, Groth (mini-boss), kobold sentries, Skree (mini-boss, negotiable), the basilisk (boss). Two real faction forces, not a monolith.
- **Traps/hazards**: dwarven pressure-plate traps (repurposed, in-fiction logical), a gas pocket (environmental, not a "trap" dressed up), the Shaft's fall risk.
- **Puzzles/exploration**: the vault's motto-password, the sanctuary's ward, the cistern sabotage discovery, the armory cache.
- **Treasure/secrets**: Groth's runed morningstar (story item), the vault's runed dagger (story item), the shrine boon, the sanctuary itself, the foreman's log (strategic advantage), the tainted cistern (a lever the party can pull on the standing faction war, not just loot).

## Phase 4 — Running it

**Resting pressure**: only room 13 (The Sealed Refuge) supports a real long rest. Elsewhere, a rest risks a patrol or a faction skirmish finding the party — don't railroad an interruption every time, but don't let the party treat any random corridor as equally safe.

**Sound and atmosphere**: distant, irregular hammering (kobolds still working old veins out of habit even without a foreman), the reek of old iron and bugbear refuse near the keep, dripping water throughout the mine levels, and — near room 19-20 specifically — an unnatural quiet, no hammering, no vermin, nothing that lives near the basilisk by choice.

**The standing conflict is a lever, not scenery.** The party can let the kobolds and bugbears bleed each other, back one side against the other (Skree is genuinely willing to deal; Groth is not, but his second-in-command might be if Groth falls), or clear the site themselves and hold it outright. All three are real, supported outcomes — this isn't a scripted "help the good guys" setup, both sides are exactly as self-interested as the party.

# The Weeping Tower

**Where**: A day west of Gallowmere, in marsh country — a leaning stone tower that condenses water down its walls constantly, hence the name
**Difficulty**: Level 3-5
**Type**: Small dungeon — arcane treasure, a bound guardian, and a personal thread for Ilvaneth

**⚠ Two-character party**: this range assumes four PCs. For Ilvaneth and Kriv read it as roughly **+2 levels** — see `../two-player-scaling.md`.

## What it is

The tower of a wizard named **Ovelt Harn**, dead some sixty years. Harn spent his career on a single question: whether a mind can be preserved intact past the body's death without becoming one of the shambling, degraded things necromancy usually produces. He did not want undeath. He wanted continuity.

He failed. The tower is what he left behind.

## What's in it

**The danger**: Harn bound something to guard his work — a construct or a bound spirit, still functioning, still following instructions from a man sixty years dead. It is not hostile on sight; it challenges intruders and demands they identify themselves as students or thieves. Lying to it is possible. It fights only when the answer is unsatisfactory. There are also the failed attempts: two or three of Harn's earlier experiments, which did degrade, sealed in the lower rooms and still moving.

**The treasure**: Harn's **spellbook** (a genuine haul for Ilvaneth — several spells above her current level, transcription costs and all), alchemical supplies, and his **research notes**: a detailed, honest, methodical account of every approach to preserving the self past death and exactly how each one failed. Not a lich's grimoire. A map of dead ends, written by someone who was trying to do it *right*.

## Why it matters beyond loot

This is **Ilvaneth's site**. Her stated long-term goal is to transcend death — not for power, but to never be nothing again. The Weeping Tower is the campaign telling her: *someone smarter than you spent forty years on this and left you his failures*.

The notes give her real, mechanical progress toward her goal and a real warning at the same time. They also point at the one method Harn never got to test — because it required something no wizard could manufacture: a relic that had already held the pattern of a self past death. Something like a piece of a crown made from the ashes of kings.

That connection should be discoverable but not stated outright. Let her find it.

## The interweave

A wizard's tower with loot in it, which is also the moment Ilvaneth's personal ambition and the main plot become the same object. She came here for a spellbook. She leaves understanding that the thing in her pocket is the missing variable in a sixty-year-old experiment.

# The Ironclad Compact

Built to the standard in `.claude/rules/faction-design.md`. Absorbs and expands `faction-subplots.md`'s "the personal grievance" section (kept there, not duplicated) and `dm-notes.md`'s original one-line summary.

---

## Phase 1 — Identity & Belief

**Public position**: Pure commerce. Sell to all sides, keep the war profitable, never let anyone win.

**True belief vs. cover**: Almost entirely sincere, which is what makes the Compact easy to trust and hard to like. Coren Ashvale is "refreshingly honest about being a mercenary" (`npcs/coren-ashvale.md`) — there is no ideological mask to pull off, no secret doctrine underneath. The one exception is Coren himself, where the Ashvale necropolis grievance has cracked the professional-detachment cover and turned into something personal — see Phase 4.

**Founding & history**: The Ashvale line were minor nobility, three generations back, before the war's earlier turns cost them land and standing they could never recover through the usual channels — inheritance, marriage, royal favor. Rather than fade into decorative poverty the way lesser houses do, they converted what they still had — trained soldiers, fortified holdings, a name people still answered to — into a selling proposition. Ironhold, their old family seat, became company headquarters instead of an ancestral hall. The Compact's entire ethos (contracts over cause, coin over crown) is the Ashvale family's own hard-won conclusion, applied at institutional scale: legitimacy didn't save them once; competence and liquidity will.

**Alignment**: Lawful Neutral, almost definitionally — "keeps his word to the letter of the contract and not one inch past it" (`npcs/coren-ashvale.md`) describes the whole institution, not just its leader. They are not cruel for its own sake and not loyal for its own sake either; the contract is the entire moral universe.

**Relationship to the gods**: Deliberately, institutionally indifferent. The Compact has no patron deity and no doctrine — commerce doesn't require faith, and faith is bad for negotiating with all sides equally. The closest thing to religious practice is a mercenary-culture habit of swearing contracts "on the Broker's Ledger," a half-mythic figure nobody can name a temple for and nobody has bothered to confirm is real. **Given gods are genuinely active in this setting, this is either the Compact's shrewdest position (no god to disappoint, no doctrine to violate) or a real blind spot they've never had reason to examine** — they've simply never needed a god's blessing to close a contract, and it hasn't cost them anything yet.

## Phase 2 — Power & Resources

**Military/combat assets**: The most conventional and most numerous fighting force of the three factions — trained mercenary companies, garrison contracts (Gallowmere's garrison and Harrowgate's Garrison House both quietly run on Compact retainer money, per `npcs/doreth-kell.md` and `npcs/renata-kroll.md`), and Ironhold itself as a fortified company headquarters. Sethra Shestendeliath's own command is a working example of a Compact field officer with real troops (`npcs/sethra-shestendeliath.md`) — this party genuinely needs some, per `two-player-scaling.md`.

**Magical/ritual assets**: Minimal and purchased, not cultivated — the Compact hires spellcasters as contractors the way they hire swords, rather than maintaining an internal magical tradition the way the Concordat does.

**Economic resources**: The deepest treasury of the three factions by a wide margin — active revenue from contracts with all three succession claimants simultaneously, toll stations (Sallow Ford), and retainer arrangements woven into towns that don't officially know they're compromised (Gallowmere, Harrowgate). Coren's stated strategy — sell to everyone, at scale, ensure no decisive advantage — is explicitly a business model, not a passing tactic.

**Territory**: See `gazetteer.md`'s Region Control table. Ironhold (Region V, ancestral Ashvale seat) is uncontested Compact ground; the same region's Ashvale Necropolis is occupied by the Concordat, the wound at the center of Phase 4. An open chapterhouse in Karsgate; toll control at Sallow Ford; quiet retainer influence embedded in Gallowmere and Harrowgate's garrisons without either town's ordinary residents knowing the extent of it.

**Reach & influence**: The widest of the three factions, precisely because it isn't ideological — anyone with coin can hire them, which means their presence (garrison retainers, toll stations, contracted officers) is threaded through ordinary towns the Concordat and Choir never bother with.

**Signature & presentation**: No uniform ideology, but a real house style — disciplined, professional, unshowy. Compact contracts carry a hammer-and-chain seal (already seen by Dallin Marsh, `bug-log.md`/`state.md`); Compact officers negotiate in plain, itemized terms, the opposite of the Concordat's procedural cadence or the Choir's doctrinal language. A Compact soldier looks like a soldier, not a symbol.

**Typical forces — stat-block anchor**: No dedicated encounter file yet exists for a standard Compact patrol/garrison detail (unlike the Concordat's escort, `encounters/millward-road-convoy.md`) — build one to the same standard the first time the party fights or allies with Compact regulars, scaled per `two-player-scaling.md`.

## Phase 3 — Structure & People

**Hierarchy**: Warmarshal (Coren Ashvale — effectively chief executive) → regional officers/captains (Sethra Shestendeliath is a working example) → company commanders → contracted rank and file, who may not even think of themselves as "Compact" so much as employed by whichever company holds the paper that month.

**Key-NPC connection web**:
- **Coren Ashvale** trusts **Sethra Shestendeliath** as a capable regional officer — he does not know she is the surviving heir of a house the Compact itself helped destroy, which is exactly the kind of detail his own organization's contract-broker culture would have buried rather than flagged.
- **Sethra** is Kriv's sister and the party's warmest available route to Coren (`npcs/sethra-shestendeliath.md`) — "a far easier route to the Warmarshal than Factor Grine in Karsgate."
- **Captain Doreth Kell** (Gallowmere garrison) is a Compact retainer asset, not an officer — paid to look away, not commanded. The Compact already holds leverage over him (his supply skimming) and uses it as insurance rather than blackmail, which is a very Compact way to hold power: quietly, and only when it matters.
- A parallel, unnamed retainer arrangement runs Harrowgate's Garrison House, per Renata Kroll's discovery (`npcs/renata-kroll.md`) — the same pattern as Gallowmere, meaning this is standard Compact practice, not a one-off.

**Internal fractures**: The necropolis grievance (`faction-subplots.md`) — kept there as the authoritative account.

**Betrayal potential, beyond the necropolis grievance**: Any retainer asset the Compact holds by leverage rather than loyalty (Kell is the confirmed case) is a soft spot — they comply out of fear of exposure, not commitment, and would turn the instant a better, safer offer appeared. The Compact's whole retainer model runs on this same soft foundation across every town it's embedded in, not just Gallowmere.

**Relations with the other two factions**:
- **vs. Ashlord Concordat**: One grievance from open conflict, not yet there (`faction-subplots.md`). Coren wants a deniable instrument, not a war — the Compact's own commercial logic argues against open conflict even when its Warmarshal personally wants blood.
- **vs. Cinder Choir**: Mutual indifference, currently, and genuinely so — the Compact "will happily sell to them" (`faction-subplots.md`). There is no ideological friction because the Compact doesn't have an ideology to conflict with the Choir's doctrine. This could change fast if the Choir ever needed the kind of muscle only the Compact can sell.

**Recruitment & exit terms**: The most straightforward of the three factions to join, on paper — the Compact hires. A contract is a contract: clear terms, clear pay, clear duration. Leaving is equally simple *unless* you're the kind of asset held by leverage rather than choice (Kell's situation) — those "contracts" have no clean exit at all, which is the whole point of holding them that way. A PC could plausibly take direct Compact work (Coren's "deniable instrument" role is written for exactly this) without ever becoming embedded the way Sethra is.

## Phase 4 — Intelligence & Operations

**Intelligence network**: Built on retainer relationships and paid access rather than dedicated spies — garrison commanders who report troop movements and local gossip as part of what their stipend buys (Kell's "garrison intelligence" offering is the confirmed template), plus ordinary commercial intelligence any large trading operation would maintain. Less mystical than the Concordat's correspondence network, more embedded in everyday town life.

**Decision logic, at faction scale**: The Compact responds to a problem the way a business responds to a bad account — first absorb the cost quietly, then look for leverage, then escalate only if the cost of inaction exceeds the cost of action. This is precisely why Coren hasn't moved on the Concordat yet despite real personal fury: open war is bad business, so he's shopping for a cheaper solution (a deniable instrument) before he'll pay the expensive one (open conflict).

**Endgame — what winning looks like**: Not a throne. The Compact's actual win condition is a succession war that never fully resolves, or resolves in a way that leaves every claimant still needing mercenary muscle afterward. A single clear victor is the worst outcome available to them — it ends the market. This makes them structurally opposed to both the Concordat's and the Choir's actual goals without ever needing to fight either one directly over it.

**Vulnerabilities & blind spots**:
- **Coren's personal fury is not accounted for in the institution's own logic.** The Compact's whole model assumes profit-driven rationality at every level — but its own Warmarshal is one grievance away from an irrational, expensive war over a burial ground. The organization has no mechanism for restraining its own chief executive if he decides the necropolis is worth the cost.
- **The retainer model is leverage-based, not loyalty-based**, in every town it's embedded in — meaning every Compact-compromised authority figure (Kell confirmed, Harrowgate's Garrison House suspected) is a pressure point the party can turn, the same way they've already partly turned Dallin Marsh against Kell's own interests.
- **They don't know what they did.** Nobody currently active in the Compact's leadership knows the necropolis occupation and House Shestendeliath's destruction are connected through the same old contract-broker culture that treats betrayed families as line items — if that connection ever surfaced publicly (Kriv revealing himself as the Compact's own historical victim, to the Compact that currently employs his sister), it would be a genuine institutional embarrassment, not just a private grief.

**Default policy toward Crown-bearers**: Purely transactional, no default hostility or reverence — a Crown fragment is worth exactly what someone will pay for it or pay to prevent it from moving. The Compact's stance on the party specifically (once it has one) will be set entirely by what they're worth as a contract, an asset, or a threat — never by doctrine, since they have none.

**Public reputation**: Respected the way a reliable tradesman is respected — not loved, but trusted to do exactly what the contract says and nothing else. Ordinary people resent them quietly (garrison retainers are an open secret nobody enjoys) but rarely fear them the way they fear the Concordat's undead escorts or the Choir's doctrine; a Compact soldier is a known, priced quantity.

## Phase 5 — Prophecy & Fate

**Prophecy/destiny angle**: None, and pointedly so — a succession war resolved by prophecy is exactly the kind of outcome the Compact's business model can't price or hedge against. If the Hollow Prophet ever turns out to be real, the Compact treats it as a market disruption to route around, not a revelation to fear or serve.

**The gods' direct stance**: None confirmed, and the Compact hasn't asked. Unlike the Concordat's "unasked question" (Phase 1/5 of `factions/ashlord-concordat.md`), which is a live, examined risk the Concordat itself doesn't want to look at too closely, the Compact's indifference is total and unexamined at every level — they would be genuinely surprised, not alarmed, if a god ever took an interest in them either way.

# The Ashlord Concordat

Built to the standard in `.claude/rules/faction-design.md`. Absorbs and expands `faction-subplots.md`'s "Sarelle vs. Orell Tain" section (kept there, not duplicated) and `dm-notes.md`'s original one-line summary.

---

## Phase 1 — Identity & Belief

**Public position**: Only ritual and ash-lineage can legitimize a sovereign. The succession war is a symptom of a solvable problem — there is no verifiable, incorruptible test of who should rule. The Concordat exists to administer that test.

**True belief vs. cover**: For the rank and file — archivists, ritualists, the honor-guard details — this is sincere. They are bureaucrats and scholars who genuinely believe correct process produces correct outcomes, the same conviction a good clerk has in a well-kept ledger. For Sarelle specifically, it has curdled into something colder: she no longer believes the test needs to crown *anyone* worth crowning, only that it needs to happen correctly, under her administration. The gap between the rank and file's sincerity and their leader's actual plan is the Concordat's central, unexamined rot.

**Founding & history**: The Concordat was chartered four generations ago, in the aftermath of a succession dispute that split the old Ember Crown clergy in two — one half insisted only a priest could judge a claimant's worth; the other insisted worth could be demonstrated through unbroken lineage and correct rite, with or without clerical blessing. The second half won the argument, broke away, and became the Concordat: scholars and ritualists who catalogue bloodlines, relics, and precedent instead of praying over them. They were tolerated rather than embraced — a faction born from a schism is never fully trusted by the side it split from, and the orthodox temple still treats them as cousins who left the family business for something colder.

**Alignment**: Lawful, officially and mostly in practice — but Lawful in service to *process*, not to any person, principle, or god. Individual members range from genuinely Neutral (the archivists who just want the record kept straight) to what Sarelle has become: Lawful Evil in substance, still speaking the language of procedure.

**Relationship to the gods**: This is the Concordat's defining wager, now that gods are confirmed real and active. **Ossara, Keeper of Names** — goddess of death, memory, and lineage — is the deity the orthodox Ember Crown temple serves (Prior Aldric Venn's temple in Harrowgate, Sister Mave's shrine in Thornwick, the still-unclaimed Cinderpath Shrine in Gallowmere are all hers). The Concordat does not worship her. They use the same conceptual territory — ash, death, lineage, the keeping of names — but claim their ritual authority comes from *correct procedure itself*, not from her blessing. This is not stated as heresy by the orthodox temple (the schism was doctrinal, not a declared break with the goddess), but it is a real, live gamble: **the Concordat has never actually tested whether Ossara cares that they've built an entire legitimacy apparatus out of her domain without asking her.** No confirmed instance of divine intervention for or against them exists yet — see Phase 5.

## Phase 2 — Power & Resources

**Military/combat assets**: No standing army in the conventional sense — their force projection is smaller, specialized honor-guard details built around **undead soldiery** (reanimated remains, raised and bound under ritual oath rather than raw necromancy) escorted by living **priest-ritualists** who maintain the binding. A full escort detail is the party's own two encounters' worth of proof: 9 skeletons + 3 priests (session 3), 6 skeletons + 2 priests (session 6) — both wiped out, both prompting the operational hardening now in effect (`campaign-clock.md`, `consequences.md`).

**Magical/ritual assets**: Ash-magic — necromancy and preservation ritual bent toward archival and legitimacy purposes rather than raw combat. Containment/alarm sigils on transported relics (bypassed twice by the party via Arcana, not yet by force). The Ashvale Necropolis is their primary working ritual site.

**Economic resources**: Institutional, not commercial — funded by old endowments, relic-appraisal fees, and (per Compact suspicion, unconfirmed to the party) quiet tithes from claimants seeking their eventual endorsement. They do not compete on coin the way the Compact does; they compete on legitimacy, which is worth more to the right buyer.

**Territory**: See `gazetteer.md`'s Region Control table — active excavation presence in the Thornlands (not yet territorial control), a contested chapterhouse in Karsgate, and occupied ground at Ashvale Necropolis (Region V), which is the live wound in their relationship with the Ironclad Compact.

**Reach & influence**: Wherever there is a relic, a bloodline record, or a succession claim worth verifying. Their name carries real weight in any city with an old archive; it means less in border country like the Marches, where nobody has bothered to ask them anything in a generation.

**Signature & presentation**: Grey and ash-toned formal dress, deliberately unornamented — Sarelle's own "dressed like a records clerk, not a magus" is the template, not an exception. Titles follow a clerical-scholarly ladder (Magus, Archivist-Magus). Their honor guards move in formation and do not speak; their priests do, and do so in the flat, procedural cadence of someone reading a filed report aloud. Concordat-sealed documents carry a stamped ash-grey sigil — a broken circle, representing the Crown's own fracture.

**Typical forces — stat-block anchor**: A standard fragment-escort detail, pre-hardening baseline, is documented in `encounters/millward-road-convoy.md` and the (unfiled) session-3 convoy fight — skeletons + priests, scaled to the value of what's being moved. **Post-hardening (day 42 onward, per `consequences.md`)**: escorts are now larger, travel with decoy routes, and keep timing secret — any new escort encounter file should be built harder than either of the first two, not identical.

## Phase 3 — Structure & People

**Hierarchy**: Archivist-Magus (Sarelle's rank, effectively supreme within the Crown project specifically, though not necessarily the Concordat's overall head) → Magus (senior archivists with independent authority, e.g. Orell Tain) → journeyman archivists and ritualists → priest-ritualists (field-capable, maintain the undead bindings) → bound honor guard (not personnel in any meaningful sense — assets).

**Key-NPC connection web**:
- **Sarelle Duskbourne** (Archivist-Magus, effective leader of the Crown project) is professionally correct toward **Orell Tain** and has been for years — she does not hate him, she considers him a valuable, wrong colleague, which is worse for him than open enmity would be.
- **Orell Tain** relies on quiet allies within the archive's own junior ranks to keep track of what Sarelle's project is actually doing — people who agree with him but haven't yet risked saying so openly. None named yet; a natural place for the party to find a second Concordat contact if Tain vouches for them.
- Sarelle's unnamed **field investigator** (dispatched day 42 per `consequences.md`'s Pattern-of-Losses threshold) reports to her directly, not through the normal archive hierarchy — a sign she's treating this as personal, not routine.
- **Sefwyn Marrow** (the Grey Tally) works for the Concordat via intermediaries, per `npcs/sefwyn-marrow.md` — Sarelle does not know Sefwyn is skimming for themselves on the side.

**Internal fractures**: Sarelle vs. Orell Tain, as established in `faction-subplots.md` — kept there as the authoritative account, not restated here.

**Betrayal potential, beyond Tain**: Any journeyman archivist who has actually read the same historical sources Tain has is a latent second Tain — the Concordat's own scholarly culture is what produces its internal dissent, which means the fracture is structural, not personal to one man. A junior archivist could be built as exactly this the first time the party needs a second inside contact.

**Relations with the other two factions**:
- **vs. Ironclad Compact**: Not yet open war, one grievance away from it (per `faction-subplots.md`) — the Concordat's occupation of Ashvale Necropolis is a live wound for Coren Ashvale specifically. The Concordat, for its part, considers the Compact's objection an inconvenience, not a threat; they have not yet grasped how personally Coren takes it. Ilvaneth's forged evidence (`consequences.md`, Pending Resolutions) is aimed directly at pushing this from "not yet open" to open.
- **vs. Cinder Choir**: Doctrinal enemies, and the sharper of the two rivalries. The Choir considers the entire Crown project heretical — crowning someone who never died and returned. The Concordat considers the Choir's doctrine unfalsifiable superstition dressed as theology, which is a particularly cutting insult from a faction whose own legitimacy claim is "correct procedure over divine sanction."

**Recruitment & exit terms**: The Concordat does not recruit outsiders as members in the way the Compact hires or the Choir converts. What they offer instead — confirmed by Sarelle's own established M.O. (`npcs/sarelle-duskbourne.md`) — is **sponsorship of a candidate**: wealth, protection, and information, extended to someone she believes could complete the Crown's final assembly on her terms. This is not membership; it is being made an instrument, dressed as an honor. There is no formal "leaving" — a sponsored candidate who refuses becomes, at minimum, a loose end who knows too much.

## Phase 4 — Intelligence & Operations

**Intelligence network**: Paid informants in towns near known or suspected relic sites (Fennick Orle in Gallowmere is the party's own confirmed example), supplemented by the Concordat's own scholarly correspondence network — archivists across the Hollow Kingdoms trade information about relic sightings and bloodline records as a matter of professional habit, which doubles as an intelligence apparatus without anyone needing to call it one.

**Decision logic, at faction scale**: The Concordat responds to a threat to the Crown project with **escalating institutional attention, not immediate violence** — first inquiry, then resources, then a dedicated investigator (exactly the sequence just played out against the party, `consequences.md`). Open force is a late resort, reserved for a confirmed, located, non-negotiable threat. This is a direct structural consequence of their own self-image: they are administrators, not soldiers, and they under-react to danger right up until the moment they can no longer justify calling it anything else.

**Endgame — what winning looks like**: A completed Crown, administered (not necessarily worn) by Sarelle or her chosen successor within the archive, with a sponsored candidate having paid the Crown's actual price on her behalf. The Concordat as an institution outlives whoever sits on the throne — that is, structurally, the entire point.

**Vulnerabilities & blind spots**: 
- **Institutional slowness.** Their escalation is procedural and therefore predictable — the party has already exploited this twice (two convoys wiped out before serious countermeasures existed).
- **The buried thing.** Proof that Sarelle began excavating months before the Emperor's death exists in the Ashvale archives (`faction-subplots.md`) — if it surfaced, it would not just embarrass her, it would hand Tain's faction a fracturing argument with hard evidence behind it for the first time.
- **The unasked question.** They have built their entire legitimacy claim on ash-ritual and lineage without ever confirming Ossara's actual position on it. If gods are real and she has an opinion, nobody in the Concordat currently knows what it is — a genuine unknown, not a controlled risk.

**Default policy toward Crown-bearers**: Recruitment first, per Sarelle's own established approach — an invitation before soldiers, a willing candidate being worth more than a corpse to loot. This is the faction-level default every individual Concordat NPC's own Decision logic should trace back to, per `npc-consistency.md`, unless a specific NPC has a stated reason to deviate (Sarelle's field investigator, dispatched specifically because recruitment isn't possible against an unidentified enemy, is the current exception).

**Public reputation**: Respected more than liked — competent, humorless, and slightly cold in the popular imagination. Nobody tells Concordat jokes. In border country like the Marches they're barely a rumor; in old archive cities they're a known, faintly resented institution, the tax assessor of relic law.

## Phase 5 — Prophecy & Fate

**Prophecy/destiny angle**: None of their own — the Concordat's entire identity is a rejection of prophecy-based legitimacy in favor of procedural legitimacy. This is precisely what makes them the Cinder Choir's opposite number, not a variant of the same idea.

**The gods' direct stance**: Unconfirmed, deliberately — see Phase 1's "unasked question." No instance of Ossara or any other deity acting for or against the Concordat has occurred in the fiction yet. If this ever needs to resolve (a cleric's spell behaving strangely near Concordat ritual, a sign at a desecrated site), it should land as a genuine, earned moment, not a casual aside — the same discipline `cinematic-moments.md` already applies to first arrivals and boss fights.

# The Cinder Choir

Built to the standard in `.claude/rules/faction-design.md`. Absorbs and expands `faction-subplots.md`'s "the doctrine problem" section (kept there, not duplicated) and `dm-notes.md`'s original one-line summary.

---

## Phase 1 — Identity & Belief

**Public position**: Only one who has died and returned may rule. Everyone else is a heretic.

**True belief vs. cover**: Sincere at the cell level, compromised at the top — and the gap between the two is the Choir's real secret, deeper than any single hidden kiln. Odric Fenn (`npcs/odric-fenn.md`) is the confirmed template: "sad, certain, and utterly reasonable in his own telling," a true believer who reached his doctrine through honest, years-long observation of the dead, not fanaticism for its own sake. Leadership, meanwhile, has spent years failing to find a genuine returner and has quietly started **manufacturing** one instead — a direct doctrinal betrayal that most cells, including Fenn's own, only half-trust and don't fully see. Fenn's file says it plainly: "he doesn't fully trust his own superiors either."

**Founding & history**: The Choir split from the orthodox Ember Crown temple during the same schism-era period the Concordat traces its own founding to, but over a different, sharper question. A priestess — her name survives in Concordat archive fragments only as a single legible letter, "V" (per the party's own Investigation find, session 5) — claimed to have died and been sent back by Ossara herself, and declared this the *true* test of legitimacy no living claimant could fake. The orthodox temple refused to recognize the claim and refused her the authority to found a doctrine on it. She did anyway. **DM reference only, not yet revealed to the party**: her full name was Vessalyn Thorne, called "the First Hollow" by the followers who believed her. Whether Ossara actually did anything to her, or whether Vessalyn believed her own claim, is exactly the question the Choir has been unable to answer ever since — which is why they need a *second* case so badly.

**Alignment**: Ranges genuinely across the organization rather than sitting in one place. Rank-and-file cells like Fenn's read closer to Neutral — grief-adjacent, sincere, patient. Leadership, having crossed into manufacturing what it can't find, has drifted to Neutral Evil in practice while still speaking the language of doctrine. This isn't a contradiction the Choir has resolved; it's a fracture nobody at Fenn's level has been allowed to look at directly.

**Relationship to the gods**: The single highest-stakes theological claim of the three factions. The Choir's entire doctrine rests on the assertion that **Ossara, Keeper of Names**, personally chooses who returns from death — a claim the orthodox temple considers blasphemous overreach (she keeps names, she does not send them back) and the Concordat considers unfalsifiable nonsense from a faction that split off for exactly this reason. **This is deliberately unresolved and should stay that way** — it is the same live question Ilvaneth's own transcendence arc is asking, and the campaign's own design intent (`faction-subplots.md`) is that a genuine died-and-returned party member would be the single most dangerous piece of leverage available in the entire campaign. Do not answer it except through play, and only when it's been earned.

## Phase 2 — Power & Resources

**Military/combat assets**: No standing force — the Choir's power is secrecy and infiltration, not numbers. Methods run to assassination, engineered martyrdom, and prophecy rather than open combat (per `dm-notes.md`'s original summary). A cell like Fenn's runs 3-4 members total, deliberately small.

**Magical/ritual assets**: Necromantic ritual aimed specifically at genuine resurrection-with-continuity — not reanimation, which the Concordat already does routinely with its honor guards, but an attempt to bring someone back *as themselves*, which is far harder and far more dangerous. The caged thing in the Ash-Kilns' fourth kiln (`npcs/odric-fenn.md`) is the confirmed, visible cost of getting this wrong.

**Economic resources**: Modest and deliberately invisible — no treasury, no institutional wealth. Cells fund themselves through mundane cover trades (Fenn's kilns are a real, licensed operation) and route grave-ash north to leadership in quantity, which is resource and ritual material at once.

**Territory**: None held openly — see `gazetteer.md`'s Region Control table, where the Choir doesn't appear as a territorial holder at all, by design. Power is distributed across hidden cells rather than concentrated in a seat.

**Reach & influence**: Wherever the dead are handled. Cells embed naturally in funerary trades, ash-kilns, graveyards, and death-adjacent work — occupations nobody watches closely, which is precisely the point. **Two cells are now confirmed and built**: the Ash-Kilns (Gallowmere, Odric Fenn) and the Karsgate Undercity cell, led by **Brother Kessic Ambrey** (`npcs/kessic-ambrey.md`) — hidden among the Undercity's pre-dynastic burial vaults, using the city's own unclaimed dead as cover. Both sit on **Yeva Ashwright**'s regional circuit (`npcs/yeva-ashwright.md`) — the network is real and has faces now, not just an implied structure. Kessic's cell is notably thinner and more anxious than Fenn's, and is independently searching the vaults for a fragment without leadership's authorization — a live wrinkle Yeva doesn't know about either.

**Signature & presentation**: Unremarkable on purpose — Fenn's own described presence ("the kind of person a town looks straight through," permanently ash-dusted forearms, a practiced boring answer ready for curious strangers) is the template for any Choir agent, not an exception. Doctrinal communication (the correspondence the party has already read) runs in allegorical, scripture-adjacent language, the opposite of the Concordat's flat procedural cadence — where a Concordat document reads like a filed report, a Choir document reads like a half-parable.

**Typical forces — stat-block anchor**: `encounters/ash-kilns-cell.md` is the confirmed reference for a standard cell encounter. A regional coordinator or leadership-tier encounter doesn't exist yet — build one to the same standard the first time the party goes above Fenn's level.

## Phase 3 — Structure & People

**Hierarchy**: The Hollow Prophet (identity concealed — do not resolve early, per `faction-subplots.md`) → regional coordinators (**Yeva Ashwright**, covering the Marches and Thornlands — the confirmed template for this rank) → cell leaders (Fenn's rank) → 3-4 cell members each, deliberately compartmentalized so no single cell's exposure brings down the network.

**Key-NPC connection web**:
- **Odric Fenn** is Allied to the party (day 33). He reports to **Yeva Ashwright** on her regional circuit, not directly to leadership — Fenn's own doubt about "his superiors" is really doubt about what Yeva herself passes upward, which he has no way to see past.
- **Yeva Ashwright** is Fenn's actual direct connection to the wider Choir (`npcs/yeva-ashwright.md`) — she has already noticed his reports thinning since day 33 and hasn't confronted him yet. This is a live, ticking piece of unresolved tension the party's own alliance with Fenn created without anyone realizing it.
- Yeva also covers a **second, unnamed cell in Karsgate's undercity** on the same circuit — the Choir's structure is confirmed to extend beyond Gallowmere, not implied to.
- Fenn's own doubt about leadership ("he doesn't fully trust his own superiors either") is the clearest confirmed thread into the Choir's internal fracture — he is not just a turned enemy, he's a disillusioned insider, which is a richer position than either. Yeva shares the same doubt, one level up, and hasn't acted on it either — the fracture runs at least two levels deep, not just at Fenn's.
- **Prior Aldric Venn** (Harrowgate, orthodox Ember Crown temple) has named the Ash-Kilns cell as the region's real threat without knowing Fenn by name — the orthodox temple and the Choir are already in indirect, one-sided conflict through him.

**Internal fractures**: The manufactured-returner secret (`faction-subplots.md`) — kept there as the authoritative account, now placed inside the fuller doctrinal-betrayal frame established in Phase 1.

**Betrayal potential, beyond Fenn**: Fenn himself is the campaign's own proof of concept — a cell leader turned through genuine doctrinal engagement (Ilvaneth's transcendence goal, the Warden story, Venn as leverage) rather than force. **Yeva Ashwright is a live second candidate, already in motion, not hypothetical** — she already doubts leadership on her own, before the party has ever met her; the right approach could turn a regional coordinator, not just a single cell, which would expose far more of the network at once than Fenn alone ever could.

**Relations with the other two factions**:
- **vs. Ashlord Concordat**: Doctrinal enemies, and the sharpest rivalry of the three — the Choir considers the entire Crown project heretical, since it would crown someone who never died. This mirrors, from the other side, the Concordat's own view of the Choir's theology as unfalsifiable (`factions/ashlord-concordat.md`, Phase 3).
- **vs. Ironclad Compact**: Mutual indifference (`faction-subplots.md`) — the Compact will sell to the Choir the same as anyone else, and the Choir has no doctrinal objection to paying for mundane services like anyone would.

**Recruitment & exit terms**: Recruitment runs through grief and doctrine, not payment or oath — people who work closely with death (kiln-keepers, undertakers, the recently bereaved) are natural candidates, approached slowly, the way Fenn presumably was once approached himself. There is no clean exit: a cell member who wants out knows the cell's cover, its members, and possibly the fourth-kiln-shaped secret every cell almost certainly has its own version of — leaving isn't refused so much as it's simply very dangerous.

## Phase 4 — Intelligence & Operations

**Intelligence network**: Built on proximity to death itself — a cell screens every body that passes through its cover trade for "signs of return," which doubles as a passive surveillance network over an entire town's grief, rumors, and unexplained deaths. Correspondence between cells and leadership carries doctrinal updates and, per the correspondence the party already read, genuine operational intelligence (fragment counts, bloodline interests) — this is not a purely mystical organization; it keeps real records.

**Decision logic, at faction scale**: Doctrine first — is this person a heretic, a threat, or a potential vessel — before violence. But leadership's own willingness to manufacture a returner shows the doctrine bends under enough institutional desperation; a faction publicly committed to *finding* the truth has already started *making* it instead, and would very plausibly settle for a good-enough manufactured candidate and simply insist the doctrine is satisfied, if time or pressure ran short enough.

**Endgame — what winning looks like**: Install a ruler who has genuinely (or at least undeniably, publicly) died and returned — by discovery if a real one ever surfaces, by manufacture if one doesn't. The Choir does not clearly distinguish between these two outcomes at the leadership level anymore, which is the whole fracture in Phase 1 stated as a goal instead of a belief.

**Vulnerabilities & blind spots**:
- **The manufacture secret**, already known to the party and to Fenn — the single most damaging piece of leverage anyone currently holds against Choir leadership, and it's sitting with people who aren't even hostile to the Choir.
- **The gap between cell sincerity and leadership's compromise.** Fenn's doubt is not unique to him; any cell shown clear evidence of the manufacture attempts is a cell that could fracture the same way he did.
- **A real returner would be uncontrollable.** If Ilvaneth (or anyone) ever genuinely died and came back, the Choir's own doctrine says that person's authority would be *self-evidently* legitimate — including, potentially, over the Prophet. Leadership has never had to reckon with what happens if the thing they're searching for actually shows up and doesn't need their permission.
- **Ash-Warden bloodline interest** — the Choir's own correspondence (already read by the party) shows active interest in Ash-Warden bloodlines specifically, very plausibly because dragon-blooded lines are read as unusually resistant to a "true" death, making them ideal candidates. **Kriv is an Ash-Warden heir and does not know the Choir is already interested in bloodlines like his** — if this connects, it elevates him from incidental Crown-bearer to a specific object of Choir attention, independent of anything Ilvaneth does. This keeps the Choir thread live for both PCs, not just Ilvaneth's arc, per `dm-notes.md`'s "equal narrative weight" standing rule.

**Default policy toward Crown-bearers**: Heretic by default, per Fenn's own stated test — *unless* proven to have died and returned, in which case the relationship inverts completely, from hostility to something closer to reverence. Every Choir NPC's individual stance should trace back to this binary test, per `npc-consistency.md`.

**Public reputation**: Essentially invisible as an organized faction — where the Choir is noticed at all, it reads as death-cult rumor, not a serious political player, which is itself an operational asset. Nobody expects the kiln-keeper.

## Phase 5 — Prophecy & Fate

**Prophecy/destiny angle**: The Hollow Prophet's identity stays concealed — this phase organizes the mystery's *structure*, not its answer. Candidates should be seeded as Chapter 1 progresses per the existing guidance in `faction-subplots.md` (someone the party already trusts, or someone they've already written off). The "Prophet's house" line from the correspondence the party already read is the live thread connecting the Prophet to Vessalyn Thorne's own founding bloodline — whether the current Prophet claims literal descent from her, or the phrase means something else entirely, is not yet decided and shouldn't be until the investigation actually reaches that point.

**The gods' direct stance**: Deliberately, permanently unresolved unless earned through play (see Phase 1). No confirmed instance of Ossara acting for or against the Choir's doctrine exists in the fiction. This is the campaign's single highest-value piece of dramatic irony available — the Choir has been trying to prove a claim for generations that the table itself doesn't know the answer to either.

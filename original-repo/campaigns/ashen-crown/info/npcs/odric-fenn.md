# Odric Fenn

**Location**: Gallowmere — the Ash-Kilns, upriver
**Role**: Kiln-keeper (cover); leader of a Cinder Choir cell
**Disposition to party**: Allied — turned without a fight, session 5
**Status in play**: Introduced and turned session 5. See `party/state.md`'s Known to the Party for the full exchange.
**Tier**: Important — a Cinder Choir cell leader, allied to the party, sitting on a dangerous secret (the fourth kiln) and a live threat (Prior Venn) neither side has resolved yet.
**Class & Level**: Cleric 6 (Death Domain) — calm, doctrinal, not eager for a fight. AC 14, HP ~48, spell save DC 13.
**Purpose**: Built as the Ash-Kilns' Cinder Choir cell leader — tasked with being turnable rather than a straight fight, and afterward as an ally holding a dangerous shared secret (the fourth kiln) and a live threat (Venn) neither side has resolved.

## Surface

A human man in his fifties, ash-grey hair cropped close, forearms dusted permanently pale from decades of handling cremation ash. Quiet, unhurried, deliberate in his movements — the kind of person a town looks straight through. Half of Gallowmere knows him by sight; nobody thinks about him. Wears the same soot-stained apron whether he's tending a fire or talking to a stranger.

Voice: low, even, never raised. Answers questions plainly and doesn't volunteer more than asked. Not cagey in an obvious way — he's had this conversation with curious strangers before and has a practiced, boring answer ready.

## What he actually wants

**Someone must return.** Fenn genuinely, calmly believes the Cinder Choir's doctrine: the Hollow Kingdoms have no legitimate sovereign because no one has actually died and come back to claim it. He isn't a raving fanatic — he burns the town's dead for a living, has for years, and has concluded from long observation that death is the missing test everyone else is skipping. He is sad, certain, and utterly reasonable in his own telling.

He runs the kilns as a real, licensed service to Gallowmere (which is true) while quietly screening every body that comes through for signs of return, and shipping grave-ash north in quantity for the Choir's rituals.

## What he's hiding

- **The cell.** 3-4 more Cinder Choir members work the kilns alongside him, all as unremarkable as he is.
- **The screening records.** A locked box in the shed — everyone in Gallowmere the Choir has watched, and why. Town-scale blackmail material.
- **The fourth kiln.** Sealed, iron-barred, kept lit. The Choir has been trying to *manufacture* a returner rather than wait for one to appear. The attempts have not gone well — something is caged in there now, and Fenn knows exactly what it became and keeps it anyway.
- **His actual doctrine test for a stranger carrying a fragment**: not hostility on sight — he wants to know whether they've died. A Crown-bearer who hasn't is a heretic by his belief; one who has is something else entirely.

## What he can offer

- Legitimacy for the site as a licensed operation — a true cover story, not a lie
- If turned or bargained with rather than fought: a working cult cell, screening records, and a real foothold on Gallowmere's ash supply (see `info/power-base.md`)
- Information on Choir doctrine and, cautiously, hints toward the wider Choir leadership (the Hollow Prophet thread) — he doesn't fully trust his own superiors either

## Decision logic — how new information could redirect him

- If Venn moves against the cell before the party does → Fenn feels betrayed regardless of whether the party caused it, since they're the ones who told him the threat existed.
- If the party ever threatens to expose the cell or the fourth kiln → per his own file, he'd rather release what's caged there than lose the site; this is a real, not bluffed, threshold.
- If either party member is confirmed to have died and returned → shifts from ally to something closer to a true believer's reverence, per his doctrine.
- If he learns the party now holds 3 fragments (not an unspecified number) → likely increases his estimate of how serious/central they are to the Crown project, may want more from the alliance in return.

## What changes his attitude

- **Toward the party**: proof, or even just a serious answer, on the question of whether either of them has died and returned. He'll listen to a lot before he acts, as long as the conversation stays a conversation.
- **Against the party**: opening the fourth kiln without his consent, or threatening to expose the cell publicly before he's had his say. If bloodied in a fight, he retreats to the fourth kiln and would rather release what's inside than lose the site.

# Yeva Ashwright

**Location**: Mobile — travels between cells under cover as a mourning-goods merchant (shrouds, funerary ash, memorial ware)
**Role**: Cinder Choir regional coordinator for the Marches and Thornlands; one of a small number of people who reports directly to the Hollow Prophet
**Disposition to party**: Unaware of them specifically
**Status in play**: Not yet introduced
**Tier**: Important — regional Choir coordinator, one of the few people with a direct line to the Hollow Prophet; her growing doubt about leadership is a real fracture point in the Choir's structure. Tracked via `campaign-clock.md`'s "Yeva Ashwright's circuit visit to Fenn" clock.
**Class & Level**: Cleric 8 (Death Domain), though her cover keeps her from ever presenting as one. AC 14, HP ~60, spell save DC 14.
**Purpose**: Built as the Choir's regional coordinator — tasked with visiting Fenn on a day-52 clock and serving as the most likely internal figure to eventually doubt Choir leadership out loud.

## Surface

A tiefling, ash-grey horns filed smooth and unremarkable, dressed like exactly what her cover says she is — a modest, well-traveled dealer in mourning goods, the kind of visitor a grieving household is glad to see and forgets within the week. Soft-spoken, patient, professionally sympathetic in a way that reads as genuine because, in its own doctrinal sense, it is. She has sat with more freshly bereaved families than any priest in three regions and has learned exactly how to ask about the dead without ever sounding like she's asking.

## What she actually wants

**A confirmed second case, found honestly — and she is running out of patience with leadership's other method.** Yeva joined the Choir the way most cells do: through grief, decades ago, and through the doctrine's genuine promise that death could mean something other than erasure. She still believes it. What she does not fully trust is what leadership has started doing about it — she has heard enough, secondhand, to suspect the manufacture attempts, and she has chosen, so far, not to ask the question directly, because she isn't ready for either answer.

## What she's hiding

- **Her actual doubt about leadership**, from her own superiors as much as from any cell she visits — she reports faithfully and asks nothing, which is its own quiet form of complicity she hasn't examined too closely.
- **Fenn's cell has gone quiet in a way she's noticed.** Grave-ash shipments from the Ash-Kilns are still arriving on schedule, but Fenn's own reports have thinned since roughly day 33 — shorter, vaguer, missing the small operational details he used to include. She hasn't confronted him yet. She's due to visit within the next several weeks on her normal circuit.
- **She does not know about the party at all** — not that they exist, not that Fenn turned, not that he's protecting the fourth kiln's secret from her specifically now as much as from Prior Venn.

## Decision logic — how new information could redirect her

- If she visits the Ash-Kilns and finds Fenn's cover story doesn't hold up under her own close questioning → she doesn't report him immediately; she's spent too long doubting leadership herself to be quick to burn a cell leader who might have a real reason. She investigates further before deciding.
- If she learns Fenn is protecting a Crown-bearing party from exposure → this reads, to her, as *exactly* the kind of doctrinal test her own faith prepared her for (is this person a heretic or something else) — she is more likely to want to meet them herself than to immediately report upward.
- If she ever gets real evidence of the manufacture attempts (not just secondhand suspicion) → this is the one thing that could turn her against leadership entirely; she has been actively not-looking for exactly this reason.
- If ordered by leadership to remove or replace Fenn → she would slow-walk it, not refuse outright — she isn't ready to be a martyr for a cell leader she hasn't even confirmed is compromised.

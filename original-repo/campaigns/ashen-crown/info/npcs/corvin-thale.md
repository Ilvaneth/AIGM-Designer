# Corvin Thale — The Sable Casket

**Location**: The Sable Casket, Harrowgate (appointment-only antiquities dealer)
**Role**: Antiquities and curiosities dealer, regional relic-trade information broker
**Disposition to party**: Neutral, deal struck — first meeting session 4
**Status in play**: Met face-to-face session 4. Kriv showed the Barrow-King's Blade; confirmed genuine. Deal made: party shows him future ash-king/Crown-adjacent finds first (no obligation to sell), he stays silent about their identity and shares regional relic-trade rumors. Told them a client recently asked if he could describe "an elf and a dragonborn" — the party openly confirmed (via Ilvaneth) they already know they're being hunted, which Corvin took in stride rather than growing alarmed. Currently searching his network for an item comparable to the blade; 2-4 weeks, not free (given day 28, not yet due as of day 37). Ilvaneth's Insight (21): the deal itself is honest, but his fascination with the relics is personal/collector-obsessive, not purely mercenary — a long-term risk if the party over-trusts him with something truly valuable.
**Tier**: Important — a real regional information nexus (already the hinge connecting Kessra Vane's thread to the party) whose own collector-obsession is a tracked, real exposure risk.
**Class & Level**: Wizard 4 (no combat subclass — a career scholar-merchant, Identify/Detect Magic-flavored utility, not a fighter). AC 12, HP ~24.
**Purpose**: Built as Harrowgate's antiquities dealer and regional relic-trade information broker — tasked with identifying ash-king/Crown-adjacent finds, sourcing rumors, and (via Kessra Vane) serving as the hinge connecting the party to a rival fragment-hunter.

**Session 6, day 37**: party brought Kargrim's Reach loot unprompted (not ash-king/Crown-related, so outside the standing arrangement, but shown anyway). Bought Groth's morningstar (100 gp, no haggle — his own "fair price" offer) and the corroded dwarven weapons (40 gp, memorabilia value only). Personally fascinated by the harvested basilisk eyes and fangs — bought them for his own collection at 180 gp, clearly the collector in him rather than the merchant speaking. Declined the worked silver, raw silver ore, and bugbear gear as outside his specialty, pointed the party to Ironmonger's Row instead.

## Surface

Gnome, late fifties (old for a gnome, and it shows in how carefully he moves), precise and unhurried in a way that reads as controlled rather than slow. Ink-stained fingers, a jeweler's loupe on a cord around his neck, clothes fine but deliberately unshowy — he doesn't want to look like a target. Speaks like someone who has learned that silence gets better prices than eagerness.

## What he actually wants

To keep dealing in rare and dangerous things without ever being caught holding one when it matters. He doesn't sell off a shelf — every transaction is vetted, every buyer sized up before anything real is shown. He genuinely loves the objects themselves (a scholar's appetite, not just a merchant's), which is part of why he's survived this long: he knows what he's looking at.

## What he knows / can offer

- **Doesn't stock a shelf of magic items.** One real piece at a time, sourced carefully, sold to someone he trusts not to make noise with it.
- **Recognizes real craftsmanship on sight.** Ash-king era work, Concordat funerary style, Compact military issue — he can place origin and rough age accurately, which is worth more than any single item he has in stock.
- **Knows who's buying and selling relics regionally** — a genuine rumor/lead source for magic-item and Crown-adjacent threads, not a shop to browse.
- **Hears things.** Regional relic trade gossip reaches him first, including anything Concordat-adjacent — he'd notice if descriptions of "an elf and a dragonborn" were circulating, though he has no reason yet to connect that rumor to the two people in front of him unless something tips him off.

## Decision logic — how new information could redirect him

- If the client asking about "an elf and a dragonborn" turns out to be Concordat-affiliated and offers real money → he weighs the payout against the standing arrangement; his own file says he's collector-obsessive, not purely mercenary, so a genuinely rare item might tempt him more than coin would.
- If the party ever hands him something he can't resist examining (an actual Crown fragment, not just adjacent loot) → his fascination could override his usual caution, per Ilvaneth's Insight read on him — this is his established risk, not a new one.
- If he's ever directly threatened or leaned on by a faction → he protects his own position first; loyalty to the party is a business relationship, not a bond.

## First contact — how to play it

Appointment-only in principle, but Kriv's noble bearing (Position of Privilege) or a name-drop can get an audience. Corvin evaluates before he warms: sizes up the visitors, asks careful questions, reveals nothing of real value until he's decided they're worth the risk. He's transactional, not hostile — a good relationship here pays off repeatedly (regional relic gossip, future item leads), a bad one just means he stops answering the door.

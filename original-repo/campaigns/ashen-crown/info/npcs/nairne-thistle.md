# Nairne Thistle

**Location**: Cair Dunnow, the Cindermoor
**Tier**: Important — she speaks for the Moot Circle's eldest households; whether Cair Dunnow ever opens up (ash-king lore, a doctrinal correction to the Concordat/Choir, the Moor-Ghost bounty) runs through her judgment first
**Purpose**: The village's actual gatekeeper — created so Cair Dunnow's insularity (`locations/cair-dunnow.md`) has a face and a voice instead of staying an abstract village policy. Everything the party might get from Cair Dunnow gets tasked through convincing her specifically.
**Role**: Eldest of the Moot Circle's four sitting households — not a chief, a first-among-equals who speaks when the Circle agrees she should.
**Disposition to party**: Warming — the Moor-Ghost bounty was cleared for real, same-day, and a victim's medallion was returned rather than kept or sold. Still short of full trust; the ash-offering ritual is the one thing left before she'll give the doctrinal correction per her own Decision logic.
**Class & Level**: Non-combatant, flavored SRD `Commoner` block if it ever genuinely comes to blows (extremely unlikely — her whole leverage is patience and village consensus, never force). Her weight on the story runs entirely through judgment and information access, not combat capability, per `npc-tiers.md`'s carve-out for Important NPCs who are never meant to fight.

## Surface

Half-elf, easily past a hundred years old though nothing about her reads frail — a long, weathered face, white hair braided tight and pinned close to the skull (practical, not ornamental), hands rough from decades of felting wool. Dresses exactly like everyone else in the village — undyed wool, no marker of rank. Speaks slowly, chooses words like she's rationing them.

## What they actually want

To be left alone with what Cair Dunnow already has and already knows — not converted, not studied, not "discovered." She has watched every outside power (the Concordat, the Choir, the orthodox temple before either) claim a piece of what her village still practices whole, and misname it doctrine each time. She doesn't correct them. Being right and ignored for centuries is survivable; being right and *noticed* has historically ended badly for smaller traditions than this one.

## What they're hiding

That Cair Dunnow's funerary practice isn't just "older" than the Ember Crown schism — it's the actual, unbroken original, and she knows exactly how much of what the Concordat and Choir believe about their own founding doctrine is wrong. She will not volunteer this. It has to be earned, per `cair-dunnow.md`'s own rule (respect for the correct form, not threats or credentials).

The Moor-Ghost (an ettin, `regional-threats.md`) is a second, more practical lever — she is genuinely tired of losing people to it and has been too proud/insular to ask outside help until now. This is closer to the surface than the doctrinal secret and easier for the party to actually close.

## Current knowledge & stance

First contact, day 89/90, session 19. She has seen the party ride in — an elf and a dragonborn, on horses, from off the moor. Nothing about them yet confirms or denies anything; she is watching how they open the conversation before deciding anything else. She does not yet know they carry Ashen Crown fragments, hold any faction allegiance, or have any connection to the ash-kings beyond being strangers who rode up to the Moot Circle uninvited.

**Opening exchange**: the party said they're bound for Karsgate, crossed paths with the village on the road, and hoped to learn something from "scholars" like Cair Dunnow's elders. Respectful, no demand, no faction credentials, no demonstration of anything yet — squarely her second Decision-logic branch (ordinary guarded conversation, practical information free, nothing real). She's mildly wry at being called a "scholar" — Cair Dunnow doesn't think of what it does as scholarship, just as doing the correct thing properly — but the framing itself isn't offensive to her. She named that travelers have gone missing near the moor's edge over the last few months (one, then another, then a third) and asked directly whether the party is actually asking about it. **They asked "what do you know" — genuine follow-through, not a brush-off.** She named the Moor-Ghost directly (an ettin, 3 months known, never acted on out of pride/insularity, not incapacity). The party pushed back skeptically on "we won't forget" as a reward — fair challenge, and she clarified rather than took offense: Cair Dunnow has no coin economy worth offering outsiders, so "remembered" isn't sentiment, it's the actual mechanism — it's what opens the door to the real thing they came here hoping for. She told them there's a real form/ritual worth performing at the Old Hearth-Shrine, in general terms only — **she has no channel to know Ilvaneth's own Gallowmere ossuary ash-offering (session 1), and must never be voiced as recognizing or referencing it specifically.** A live-narration slip briefly had her do exactly that; retracted the same turn, see `bug-log.md` (2026-09-05, `npc-inconsistency`). Any resemblance the players draw between Cair Dunnow's rite and Ilvaneth's own past action is their own recognition, not something Nairne can ever confirm from her side. No mention of the Ashen Crown or ash-king doctrine has been made by either side yet; she still hasn't confirmed the party actually holds a fragment or has any real connection to it.

**The party offered directly to deal with the Moor-Ghost.** Per Decision logic's fourth bullet — she takes this seriously immediately, no second asking needed, and gives real directions (see next scene). This is now a live, in-progress bounty, not just disclosed information.

**Bounty cleared, same day.** The party killed the ettin and returned with its head as proof and the medallion of one of its three victims (identified by Nairne as Wenna Coll's son). Disposition moved to Warming — real gratitude, not performative. She has not yet offered the doctrinal correction (Concordat/Cinder Choir founding-history correction) — per her own Decision logic, that's gated on the ash-offering ritual at the Old Hearth-Shrine too, not the Moor-Ghost alone.

**The ash-offering ritual was performed, genuinely.** Ilvaneth burned a private childhood keepsake (her own first pickpocketing take, kept since — a real, costly, honest offering) and spoke honestly about it at the Old Hearth-Shrine. Both conditions in Nairne's Decision logic are now met. She owes the party the doctrinal correction the next time it comes up in conversation — a real "you've earned this" moment, not a data dump, and not something to withhold further or make them ask twice for.

**Doctrinal correction delivered, day 90.** Checked against Sylvenna's (`npcs/sylvenna.md`, day 69) prior corrective lore first — deliberately not repeated. Nairne's own, new contribution: the Ashen Crown was originally a mourning-relic, not a symbol of dominion — whoever genuinely accepted a real loss could carry it. An unnamed ash-king inverted this: loss became power, acceptance became a claim to rule. Both the Concordat's ash-magic and the Cinder Choir's death-obsession are built on that inverted, later version — neither faction knows the original meaning, because only Cair Dunnow kept it. She explicitly acknowledged Sylvenna's info as accurate rather than duplicating it, and offered this as the piece Sylvenna wouldn't have: *why* the Crown started, not just what's wrong with its founders. Ties directly to The Drowned Cathedral's Room 9-10 finding (day 87) that reassembly requires a living vessel/transformation, not a power transfer — the same "acceptance, not acquisition" logic, now confirmed from a second, independent, much older source.

## Decision logic

- If the party leads with threats, faction credentials (Concordat/Choir), or a demand — she gives nothing beyond directions out of the village. Cair Dunnow doesn't argue with outsiders, it just stops answering.
- If the party is plainly respectful and patient, without yet demonstrating anything — she engages in ordinary, guarded conversation. Practical village information (where to camp, what not to touch) is free. Anything real is not.
- If the party performs the ash-offering ritual correctly at the Old Hearth-Shrine, unprompted — real attention, per `cair-dunnow.md`. This is the fastest path to anything substantive.
- If the party offers to deal with the Moor-Ghost — she'll take that seriously and name it directly, without needing to be asked twice; it's a real, current problem, not a test she's dangling to see if they're clever enough to guess it exists.
- If both the ritual and the Moor-Ghost are addressed — she'll go as far as the doctrinal correction, treated as a genuine "you've earned this" moment, not a data dump.

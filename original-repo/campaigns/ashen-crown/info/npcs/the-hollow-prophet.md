## DM truth — who she actually is

**Ilyra Thorne.** A shadar-kai, appearing ageless the way her people often do — DM discretion on exact years, but old enough to have led the Choir for decades. She is the great-great-granddaughter of **Vessalyn Thorne**, "the First Hollow," the priestess who founded the Choir generations ago after the orthodox temple refused to recognize her death-and-return claim (`factions/cinder-choir.md`, Founding & history). The title "Hollow Prophet" has passed down the Thorne bloodline ever since — **inherited authority, not personally earned**. This resolves `factions/cinder-choir.md`'s open Phase 5 question ("whether the current Prophet claims literal descent from her... is not yet decided") — she does, and she has never told anyone outside her innermost circle.

**The doctrinal problem this creates, which she has never spoken aloud**: Ilyra has never died and returned. Her authority rests entirely on bloodline and doctrine-interpretation, not on the one proof her own faith says matters most. This is the same shape of problem Sarelle Duskbourne has with the Crown itself (a leader who needs someone *else* to complete the thing that would validate her position) — a deliberate thematic mirror across two of the three factions. She has read her own doctrine as carefully as Orell Tain reads the Concordat's, and she knows exactly what it says about a leader who hasn't done the one thing that matters.

## Surface

Shadar-kai, grey-skinned, quiet in the specific way of someone who has spent a very long time being listened to rather than needing to raise her voice. Dresses plainly, no obvious symbols of office — the followers who meet her in person already know who she is; she has no need to perform it. Speaks slowly, chooses doctrine over sentiment, and treats grief (her followers' and her own) as the most honest thing a person can bring her.

## What she actually wants

**A confirmed returner, found honestly, before the manufacture program produces something the doctrine can't survive.** She authorized the manufacture attempts — the Ash-Kilns' fourth kiln and whatever Kessic Ambrey's cell has quietly tried in the Undercity — not out of impatience alone, but because she has done the math Yeva Ashwright is only starting to suspect: the Choir is generations old, has never once produced a genuine case, and her own bloodline's claim to leadership grows thinner with every year that passes without proof. She would take a genuine returner over a manufactured one without hesitation. She is not certain, anymore, that one will ever come.

## What she's hiding

- **Her bloodline connection to Vessalyn Thorne** — known only to a handful of the Choir's innermost circle, never to regional coordinators like Yeva.
- **That she herself has never died and returned.** If this became widely known inside the Choir, it would not necessarily end her leadership (bloodline succession is its own doctrine, separately defensible) but it would hand every doubting coordinator — Yeva Ashwright specifically — a concrete reason to stop reporting faithfully and start asking questions out loud.
- **She authorized the manufacture program.** Fenn and Kessic both believe they're operating with some degree of institutional distance from leadership's full knowledge. They aren't. She knows about both kilns, in general terms, though not their specific recent history with the party.
- **A real returner would be uncontrollable, and she knows it** (`factions/cinder-choir.md`, Phase 4) — including, potentially, over her. She has thought through what happens if the thing she's spent her life searching for actually appears and answers to no one, including her, and has no clean answer.

## Decision logic — how new information could redirect her

- **If a genuine returner is ever confirmed anywhere in the Hollow Kingdoms** (including, potentially, Ilvaneth) → this overrides every other consideration instantly. Per doctrine, that person's legitimacy is self-evident — she would move to make contact personally, not delegate it, and would treat them with a deference she extends to no one else alive.
- **If her bloodline secret is ever exposed** → she doesn't panic or lash out; she reframes it doctrinally, arguing inherited stewardship of the doctrine is itself a legitimate form of authority distinct from the return-test. Whether the Choir's own membership accepts that reframing is a live, unresolved question — a genuine crack in her authority if it's ever tested.
- **If she learns the manufacture program produced something genuinely dangerous** (the fourth kiln's caged failure, or worse) → orders it destroyed and buried, not studied further; she is a believer, not a researcher chasing a result at any cost — this is the line she hasn't crossed yet.
- **If she learns a cell leader (Fenn, Kessic) is protecting outsiders who hold Crown fragments** → treats it exactly as Fenn himself predicted: not automatic hostility, but the doctrine's actual test applied in real time — has either of them died and returned? If not, heretics by definition, but heretics worth talking to before being condemned, per the Choir's own stated posture toward the party (`dm-notes.md`'s Factions & Reputation: Hostile by default, but can be turned).

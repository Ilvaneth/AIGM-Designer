**Role**: Professional relic-hunter and bounty broker — runs a small, disciplined crew (3-4 hired specialists, composition unconfirmed) on contract work for wealthy private clients, not any of the three factions directly
**Disposition to party**: Unaware of them specifically — not yet a confrontation
**Status in play**: Surfaced via World Events roll, day 63, session 14 (d12=9, "Other actors — a rival surfaces"). Not yet met in-fiction.

## Surface

A tiefling, lean and precise, dresses like a merchant rather than an adventurer — good boots, no visible weapons, nothing that reads as a threat until it's too late to matter. Speaks in the measured, unhurried tone of someone who has never needed to raise their voice to be taken seriously. Pays cash, keeps records, burns no bridges unnecessarily — reputation is currency in this trade, and Vaal's is spotless.

## What they actually want

Money and reputation, in that order — Vaal takes contracts from private collectors and, occasionally, minor nobility wanting a specific relic recovered discreetly, no questions asked about provenance. Currently working a contract (client's identity unconfirmed even to Vaal's own crew — a deliberately anonymous arrangement) to locate and recover "an ash-era relic fragment, funerary in origin" — which lines up uncomfortably well with the Ashen Crown, though Vaal has no idea what it actually is or does.

## Current knowledge & stance

- **As of day 63**: chasing a specific lead — the forged rumor Ilvaneth planted through Fennick Orle back on day 33 (a fragment supposedly sold in Karsgate) has reached Vaal's ears through the relic-trade grapevine, several links removed from its actual origin. Vaal is currently working that trail in Karsgate and finding it doesn't lead anywhere real — growing suspicious the lead itself may be deliberately planted misdirection, though has no idea who planted it or why.

## Decision logic

- If the Karsgate lead keeps dead-ending → Vaal starts working backward, trying to identify who benefits from a false trail existing at all. This could eventually point toward Fennick Orle, and from him, toward the party — a slow-burn thread, not an immediate confrontation.
- If Vaal and the party ever cross paths directly (same site, same lead) → professional, not hostile by default. Vaal would rather negotiate, trade information, or split a job than fight — violence is bad for business and reputation. Treats the party as potential competition to route around, not enemies to eliminate, unless they escalate first.
- If Vaal's crew is directly threatened or a job is sabotaged → becomes a genuine, resourced adversary. A crew this disciplined doesn't scatter like Orna Threk's did.
- If offered a better deal than the anonymous client is paying → mercenary loyalty is negotiable. Vaal has no personal stake in any particular outcome, only in getting paid and staying alive to spend it.

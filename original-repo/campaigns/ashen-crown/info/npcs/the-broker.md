# "The Broker"

**Location**: Ironhold — the Broker's Chapel, and the Paymaster's Hall
**Role**: Ironhold's dirty-contract exchange, operating through a religious shrine
**Disposition to party**: Unaware of them specifically
**Status in play**: Not yet introduced
**Tier**: Important — runs Ironhold's entire dirty-contract exchange under a religious cover; whether he's exposed touches both the mercenary underworld and an unresolved question about the office's own nature.
**Class & Level**: Rogue 5 (no subclass — meticulous, careful, not built to be noticed in any sense, combat included). AC 13, HP ~33.
**Purpose**: Built as Ironhold's dirty-contract exchange (the Compact-world equivalent of Corla Vint) — tasked with arranging jobs touching mercenary/Compact circles, and carrying an open DM-only question about whether "the Broker" is more than a human tradition.

## Surface

No one in Ironhold claims to know who "the Broker" actually is — leave a sealed request and a coin offering at the Broker's Chapel, and days later, someone finds you with an answer. Whether this is a real, active deity taking a genuine interest in mercenary contracts, or simply whoever currently runs the racket behind the myth, is a live question nobody in Ironhold has ever definitively settled — including, per Phase 1 of `factions/ironclad-compact.md`, the Compact itself, which has never checked.

## DM truth — not to be resolved lightly

**The current Broker is Ostren Vale**, a senior clerk at the Paymaster's Hall — a quiet, unremarkable half-elf who's spent twenty years processing mercenary pay and noticed, early on, exactly how much dirty money already flows through his ledgers unquestioned. Routing a contract fee through as a "signing bonus" or a "hazard stipend" is trivial for someone who already controls that paperwork.

**He inherited the position, not invented it.** Ostren learned the practice from his own predecessor at the Paymaster's Hall, who learned it from the one before that — the office of "Broker" has quietly passed from clerk to clerk for longer than anyone currently working there can trace. **Whether this makes "the Broker" a real, continuous entity in some sense (a role that outlives whoever holds it, not unlike how the Choir's Hollow Prophet or the Concordat's Archivist-Magus function) or just a long-running human tradition with no deeper truth is a genuine, deliberately open question** — the same category of uncertainty as the Concordat's unasked question about Ossara. Don't answer it unless the party's own investigation genuinely earns an answer.

## What he actually wants

**To keep the ledger balanced, literally and otherwise.** Ostren doesn't relish the violence he arranges — he thinks of it the way he thinks of hazard pay: an unpleasant but necessary cost of a mercenary company's business, better handled quietly and correctly than left to chance. He takes real pride in never once, in twenty years, being connected to the office he holds.

## What he's hiding

- **His identity, obviously** — even his own wife doesn't know he's the current Broker.
- **He keeps meticulous records**, unlike Corla Vint's deliberate memory-only approach — a clerk's instinct he's never been able to fully override, kept in a cipher only he can read, hidden inside genuine payroll archives at the Paymaster's Hall.
- **He has quietly declined at least one Compact officer's request** in the past — specifically, anything that would visibly destabilize the company's own command structure. He serves mercenaries' personal contracts, not Ironhold's internal politics.

## What he can arrange

The same category of work as Corla Vint in Karsgate — theft, kidnapping, intimidation, assassination — but exclusively for or involving people connected to the Compact's own world (soldiers, officers, contractors, rivals), reflecting his actual access and clientele.

## Decision logic — how new information could redirect him

- If approached at the Chapel through the proper offering-and-request method → responds through an intermediary, never in person, at least for a first contact.
- If someone tries to identify him directly through investigation at the Paymaster's Hall → he is genuinely careful, twenty years careful, and would notice being investigated well before most would notice him noticing.
- If asked to arrange something against the Compact's own command stability → declines, per his established limit.
- If exposed publicly → the "office" of Broker would very plausibly continue under someone else, per its own inherited-not-invented nature — removing Ostren doesn't necessarily end the practice, a fact he's aware of and finds strangely comforting rather than threatening.

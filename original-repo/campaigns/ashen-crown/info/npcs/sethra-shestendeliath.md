# Sethra Shestendeliath

**Location**: Ironhold, or wherever the Compact's northern operations need an officer
**Role**: Kriv's older sister. Ironclad Compact officer.
**Disposition to party**: Unknown — she doesn't know he's alive either
**Status in play**: Not yet introduced
**Level range**: Reachable level 6-8, via the Compact ladder
**Tier**: Important — a Compact captain and Kriv's sister; her own hunt for Vharkoss and her stance on the house's restoration are both live, self-directed threads independent of the party.
**Class & Level**: Fighter 7 (Battle Master) — commands soldiers, per her own file, and fights like someone who made herself indispensable inside a mercenary company on merit. AC 17 (half plate), HP ~72, Maneuver DC 15.
**Purpose**: Built as Kriv's surviving sister who joined the Compact that destroyed their house — tasked with being both a warm route to Coren Ashvale and the campaign's sharpest personal-thread complication (she doesn't want the house restored).

## Who she is

Red-scaled, like Kriv. Eight years older. She was old enough to remember the house standing, and old enough to understand exactly what happened to it.

She survived the night House Shestendeliath fell. She spent four years surviving badly afterward. Then she walked into an Ironclad Compact recruiting house and signed on with the organization that had supplied the swords that killed her parents.

She is now a **captain**, competent and respected, and she has never once told anyone her real name.

## Why she did it

Ask her and she'll tell you plainly, without apology:

> *"They were the strongest thing in the room. I was eleven and I had nothing. You don't beat a thing like that from outside — nobody does. So I got inside it."*

She is not a traitor to the house the way Vharkoss is. She didn't sell anyone. She made a calculation that a Neutral Evil character will recognize instantly, because **it is the same calculation Kriv and Ilvaneth made in the orphanage** — the world takes what it wants and calls it fair, so do the taking yourself.

## The problem she poses

**She does not want the house restored.**

Fifteen years inside the Compact taught her what Kriv is only starting to suspect: that noble houses, oaths, and legitimacy are theatre, and the people who own the swords own everything regardless of what any decree says. She has built a real position through real work. Going back to being a Shestendeliath means trading actual power for a name and a grievance.

She'll say so. She'll mean it. And she'll be at least half right, which is what makes her dangerous to Kriv's arc rather than merely inconvenient.

## What she knows

- The night itself, from the inside — she was there and she remembers who opened the gate
- **She suspects Vharkoss.** She doesn't know he's alive or where. If Kriv finds him first and tells her, she will move on it
- Compact internal politics, including Coren Ashvale's fury about the necropolis — **she is a far easier route to the Warmarshal than Factor Grine in Karsgate**
- She does *not* know about the Ash-Warden charge or the fragments. Their parents didn't tell an eleven-year-old

## What she offers

- **The Compact route to Coren Ashvale**, warm rather than cold
- Real military support — she commands soldiers, and `two-player-scaling.md` says this party desperately needs some
- A mirror. Everything Kriv is, aimed at a different conclusion

## What she costs

- She will argue against the restoration at every step
- Her loyalty to the Compact is genuine and current, not a cover story. If Kriv's ambitions and the Compact's contracts collide, she does not automatically choose blood
- **If Kriv invokes the house oath on her**, it works — she's oath-sworn too. And it will not be forgiven. Compelling his own sister is a line the campaign should make him feel

Playable now, if Kriv is ever in contact with her: she mentions, almost offhand, that she's "been hearing a name in Karsgate that doesn't sit right."

# Faction Sub-Plots

The three factions were originally written as monoliths that all want the same object. They shouldn't be. Each has internal fractures the party can exploit, ally with, or fall into.

---

## Ashlord Concordat — the succession crisis inside the succession crisis

**Full faction depth (structure, resources, intelligence network, gods, endgame) now lives in `info/factions/ashlord-concordat.md`, built to `.claude/rules/faction-design.md`'s standard.** This section stays as the authoritative account of the Sarelle/Tain fracture specifically — the new file absorbs it by reference, doesn't duplicate it.

**Public position**: Only ritual and ash-lineage can legitimize a sovereign. They are assembling the Crown to administer that test.

### The fracture: Sarelle vs. Orell Tain

**Magus Orell Tain** is a senior Concordat archivist who considers the Crown project reckless — not on moral grounds, but scholarly ones. He has read the same sources Sarelle has. He believes the historical record is unambiguous: the last ash-king assembled the Crown and what walked away wasn't him. Tain's position is that the fragments should be *catalogued and kept apart*, permanently.

He is losing this argument. He is also, quietly, correct.

**What this means for the party**:
- Tain is a potential ally *against* Sarelle, and an information source who will talk to almost anyone who shares his alarm
- He would rather the party keep their fragment and stay away than surrender it to Sarelle
- He does not know Sarelle intends to have someone else pay the Crown's price — if he learned that, he'd move against her openly

### The buried thing

Sarelle began excavating **months before** the Emperor's death. The Concordat engineered it. Proof exists in the Ashvale archives. If that surfaced, the Concordat fractures — and Tain is the man who'd use it.

→ `npcs/sarelle-duskbourne.md`, `locations/ashvale-necropolis.md`

## Ironclad Compact — the personal grievance

**Full faction depth (structure, resources, intelligence network, gods, endgame) now lives in `info/factions/ironclad-compact.md`, built to `.claude/rules/faction-design.md`'s standard.** This section stays as the authoritative account of the necropolis grievance specifically.

**Public position**: Pure commerce. Sell to all sides, keep the war profitable, never let anyone win.

### The fracture: the necropolis

The Concordat has occupied **Ashvale Necropolis** — Warmarshal **Coren Ashvale's** ancestral burial ground. For the first time in his career, something has gotten past the ledger.

He will not act openly; open war with the Concordat is bad business and his own officers would question it. So he is looking for a deniable instrument.

**What this means for the party**: This is the cleanest bridge from "two thieves with a rock" to "a force with backing." The Compact can fund them, arm them, brief them, and point them at Chapter 1's climax — and Coren holds the answer to who destroyed Kriv's house, and trades in exactly that kind of currency.

## Cinder Choir — the doctrine problem

**Full faction depth (structure, resources, intelligence network, gods, endgame) now lives in `info/factions/cinder-choir.md`, built to `.claude/rules/faction-design.md`'s standard.** This section stays as the authoritative account of the manufactured-returner fracture specifically.

**Public position**: Only one who has died and returned may rule. Everyone else is a heretic.

### The fracture: they can't find a returner

The Choir has been searching for a genuine died-and-returned candidate for years and has not found one. The **Ash-Kilns cell** in Gallowmere exists to screen the dead for signs — and, increasingly, to *manufacture* what they can't find. Those attempts have not gone well, and there is something in the deepest kiln to show for it.

A faction that cannot find its prophesied figure eventually decides to make one. That is where this is heading.

**What this means for the party**:
- They are marked by doctrine — Crown-bearers who haven't died are heretics by definition
- **But**: Ilvaneth's entire goal is transcending death. A party member who *did* die and return would flip the Choir from enemy to fanatical ally overnight. That is an extraordinary and extremely dangerous piece of leverage sitting in plain sight, and it should be available to a player clever enough to see it.

### The Hollow Prophet

Identity concealed. Do not resolve early. Seed candidates as Chapter 1 progresses — the strongest options are someone the party already trusts, or someone they've already written off.

## How the three interact

- **Concordat vs. Compact**: **Open, day 60.** Ilvaneth's forged evidence (day 39, Millward road) held — Sarelle's suspicion shifted to the Compact, per `consequences.md`'s now-resolved "Does the forged Ironclad Compact evidence hold up?" Real fighting has broken out somewhere in the Thornlands; Kriv found the aftermath of one clash en route to Shestendeliath Hold (day 60, session 14) — burned Concordat banners, a Compact-style buckle among the wreckage, no bodies. The party lit this fire and hasn't yet learned the fire is burning.
- **Concordat vs. Choir**: doctrinal enemies. The Choir considers Sarelle's whole project heresy — she intends to crown someone who never died.
- **Compact vs. Choir**: mutual indifference, currently. The Compact will happily sell to them.
- **Everyone vs. Vey**: Mother Vey has spent two years keeping all three out of Gallowmere. Every faction the party invites in costs her, and she knows it.

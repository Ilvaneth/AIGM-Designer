## What's here

Standing water, rusted ore carts long abandoned, and — scattered without any pattern, exactly where they stopped moving — **several more petrified figures**: at least one more dwarven miner, and (if the party never met them, a grim payoff; if they did, a gut-punch) shapes consistent with a stray kobold and a bugbear scout, proof neither faction's dead here were exaggeration.

The genuine, unclaimed treasure of years piles loosely around the lair — nobody who found it ever left.

### Petrifying Gaze
Any creature starting its turn within 30 ft that can see the basilisk (and isn't incapacitated) must make a **DC 12 Constitution save** unless it averts its eyes at the start of its turn (in which case it can't see the basilisk until its next turn, avoiding the save, but also can't target it with sight-based attacks). Fail: begins turning to stone, restrained, repeats the save at the end of its next turn — success ends it, second failure means **petrified** until *greater restoration* or similar.

**The foreman's log (room 19) already told the party how to survive this** — averting eyes and backing out. A party that read it can approach this fight with a real plan instead of learning the hard way.

If the basilisk ever sees its own reflection in bright light within 30 ft, it targets itself by mistake — a genuine tactical option if the party has any reflective surface and a light source to work with.

## Tactics

Slow (20 ft speed, no ranged option) — it relies entirely on the gaze to neutralize threats before they close, then finishes petrified/restrained targets with its bite at leisure. A party that manages the gaze correctly (averting, or using a reflection trick) can fight it almost like a normal CR 3 melee brute; a party that doesn't risks losing someone to stone.

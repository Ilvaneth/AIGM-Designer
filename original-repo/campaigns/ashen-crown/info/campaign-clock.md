# Campaign Clock — the world moves without them

`sandbox-index.md` says "factions act whether or not the party is watching." That was a nice sentence with no mechanism behind it. This is the mechanism.

**Use it**: at the start of every session, advance the clocks by the in-fiction time that passed, and check whether anything fired.

## How the timeline actually works — three mechanisms, not one

Built incrementally, so it's worth stating plainly in one place:

| Mechanism | Cadence | Lives in | Checked by |
|-----------|---------|----------|------------|
| **Faction clocks** | Fires at a specific estimated day (`⏳ Name — fires ~day N`) | This file, below | `clock_check.py` |
| **World Events** | Every 7 in-fiction days, scaled by elapsed time, not per-session | `world-events.md` | `world_events_check.py` |
| **Consequences** | Not day-based — fires when a cumulative Weight total crosses a stated Threshold | `consequences.md` | `consequence_check.py` |

All three run automatically via `session_state_gate.py` — none of them depend on remembering to check by hand. The day-by-day narrative log immediately below is this file's own record of what actually happened; the Faction clocks section further down is what's *scheduled* to happen next.

## When a clock fires and the party isn't there

**Most of these fire in Gallowmere. The party is currently four to five days away in Thornwick.** A clock firing off-screen is not a wasted clock — but it does need a delivery mechanism, or the consequence never reaches the table.

Three ways to land an off-screen fire, in order of preference:

1. **Let them walk into it.** The consequence is simply *true* when they next arrive. The stablemaster has already put a name to the horses; the refugee camp has already turned cold. No announcement — they discover it by acting on stale assumptions.
2. **Rumor.** A traveler on the road, a message, gossip in another town's tavern. Roll or place a situational rumor from `rumors.md`. Best for things the party would plausibly hear about.
3. **A person follows them.** Sefwyn's clock works this way by design — the consequence has legs and comes to *them*.

**Never open a session with "while you were away, X happened."** That's a DM announcement, not play. Let it be discovered.

That is **at or after** the stablemaster clock (day 18), not ahead of it. There is no route that reliably beats this clock by leaving "soon"; the party arrives right as it fires, or just after. That tension is the point; don't smooth it out, and don't fudge the arithmetic in either direction.

## Running the clock

1. **At session start**: count in-fiction days elapsed, advance every clock, check what fired
2. **When something fires**: it happens whether the party is present or not. Report it through rumor, consequence, or an NPC's changed behaviour — never as a DM announcement
3. **Preventable clocks are the point.** Alis Wend, the stablemaster, the Choir's kiln, and Kell's departure can all be stopped. A party that ignores them isn't punished arbitrarily — they simply didn't act, and the world did
4. **Log it below** when a clock fires or resets
5. **Before stating any new "X days from now" commitment in play, check it against every other pending clock's estimated date first.** Caught 2026-08-27: a Tain-meeting date (~day 42) was set without checking Corran's game (~day 40) or Roskel's summons (~day 41-43) — all three ended up clustered in the same ~3-day window in three different locations, purely by accident. Not automatically wrong (a real scheduling conflict can be good tension), but it must be a deliberate choice, not an oversight — cross-check first, then decide whether to embrace the collision or move the new date.
6. **Any "NPC will re-initiate contact" or "NPC will find the party" promise gets written here as a real pending clock** (`### ⏳ Name — fires ~day N`, an honest estimate), never left as bare prose in `state.md`'s Open Threads/Active Quests. Prose-only threads have no mechanism forcing a check — `clock_check.py` only catches a pending clock whose day has passed, and Sefwyn's Tain-introduction promise sat unmentioned for 12 days (day 25→37) specifically because it was written as "not a pending clock." See `bug-log.md`, 2026-08-27.

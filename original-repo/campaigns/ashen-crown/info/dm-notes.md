# DM Notes — Ashen Crown (spoilers, DM eyes only)

## Standing rule — this campaign has no content limits, confirmed and re-loaded every session

**Read this before designing or narrating anything.** Session Zero set no lines/veils (`Content lines/veils: None set by the table. No topics off-limits.`, below). The DM confirmed directly (2026-08-30) this is a deliberate, standing choice, not an oversight: gore, harassment/abuse themes, and unrestricted cruelty are all in scope for this table, consistent with playing two genuinely Neutral Evil protagonists. **This is not a safety-tool problem to solve by adding restrictions** — it's a standing fact that needs to be loaded at the start of every session so prep, encounters, and narration are actually built for this table, not hedged by default for a more conservative one. Design and narrate accordingly — don't soften antagonist cruelty, don't fade to black on dark consequences, don't self-censor content this table has already confirmed is welcome.

## Concept

- **Tone**: Dark fantasy, Neutral Evil protagonists — ambitious and ruthless, not cartoonishly cruel. Calculating self-interest, not chaos for its own sake.
- **Players**: 2. Both PCs Neutral Evil.
- **Starting level**: 1. Long campaign, two chapters, level 1 → 20+.

## The Hook

**The Patron + The Discovery.** Mother Vey hires the party for what looks like a simple smash-and-grab: break the seal on the old ossuary beneath Gallowmere before an Ashlord Concordat convoy arrives to claim it "officially." What's actually down there: a fragment of the original **Ashen Crown** — a relic literally forged from the cremated remains of past sovereigns, said to grant the wearer a measure of dark authority and legitimacy over the Hollow Kingdoms. Vey doesn't know exactly what it is; she just knows the Concordat wants it badly enough to send an armed convoy for it.

This is the campaign's central object across both chapters: multiple fragments of the Ashen Crown are scattered through the realm (and possibly beyond, into the Shadowfell — ash and death theming). Whoever assembles enough fragments can make a real claim on the throne, or something beyond a mortal throne entirely.

## Starting Location — Gallowmere

- **Obvious truth**: Gallowmere is a waystation for refugees and smugglers fleeing the succession war.
- **Discoverable truth**: The town garrison secretly answers to the Ironclad Compact, not the crown — Captain Kell is on a Compact retainer.
- **Secret truth**: Beneath the town lies a sealed ossuary where a fragment of the original Ashen Crown was hidden centuries ago, deliberately kept off every faction's records.

### Key NPCs

- **Mother Vey** — fence and information broker. Friendly face. Genuinely sees potential in the party; will keep working with them as long as it's profitable. Doesn't know the full value of what she's sending them after.
- **Captain Doreth Kell** — garrison commander. Neutral authority. Pragmatic, bribable, protects Gallowmere's status quo more than any faction's ideology. Secretly on Ironclad Compact retainer.
- **The Grey Tally** — rival fixer/debt-collector. Potential complication. Working the same lead on the ossuary for a different (Concordat-aligned) buyer. Can become a rival, a body, or — if the party plays it right — a source.

### Nearby Danger

An Ashlord Concordat convoy escorting an "ash-relic" (unrelated decoy relic, or so it seems) passes through Gallowmere within days of session 1 — creates a ticking clock on the ossuary job.

## Factions

### Ashlord Concordat
- **Goal**: Control the succession through ritual legitimacy — assemble Crown fragments, install a Concordat-approved sovereign (or an Archivist-Magus themselves).
- **Methods**: Ash-magic, undead honor guards, bureaucratic control of relic lore, ritual claims.
- **Leader**: Archivist-Magus Sarelle Duskbourne.
- **Disposition to party**: Neutral — useful employer if approached correctly, lethal if crossed or if the party is caught with a fragment.

### Ironclad Compact
- **Goal**: Profit. Back whichever claimant pays best; keep the war going as long as it's good for business.
- **Methods**: Mercenary contracts, coin, controlled and deniable violence.
- **Leader**: Warmarshal Coren Ashvale.
- **Disposition to party**: Neutral — purely transactional. Will hire the party, will also sell them out for the right price.

### Cinder Choir
- **Goal**: Install a ruler who has "died and returned" — true legitimacy, in their theology, requires having crossed into death and come back.
- **Methods**: Assassination, engineered martyrdom, prophecy, infiltration.
- **Leader**: The Hollow Prophet (identity concealed — a hook for later).
- **Disposition to party**: Hostile by default — treats unaffiliated Crown-seekers as heretics, unless the party can be turned or forced into their theology (a possible dark arc for one or both PCs later).

## Arc Outline

### Chapter 1 — Cinders of Succession (levels 1–10)
- **Opening (~1–3)**: The ossuary job. Party recovers the first Crown fragment, draws the attention of at least one faction, and has to decide how to play Gallowmere's local power balance.
- **Escalation (~4–8)**: Party builds a local power base — territory, muscle, informants, a reputation — while faction proxies clash around them. Rivals (starting with the Grey Tally) become real threats or are eliminated/absorbed. Level through faction lieutenant fights, monster contracts, local warlord conflicts.
- **Climax (~9–10)**: Party seizes real control of Gallowmere and its region (as warlords, crime lords, or a puppet-free power in their own right), forcing at least one major faction to treat them as a serious player rather than hired help. This is the pivot into Chapter 2.

### Chapter 2 — The Hollow Throne (levels 11–20+)
- Stakes go empire-wide. Other Crown fragments are revealed to be scattered across the Hollow Kingdoms and possibly the Shadowfell.
- The three factions' agendas collide directly with the party's ambitions — alliances of convenience, betrayals, open war.
- **Endgame options** (keep flexible, let player choice steer it): the party assembles the Crown and one/both PCs claim the throne; the party transcends mortal rule entirely (lichdom, an ascension tied to the Crown's ash-magic, a new death-god); or the Hollow Kingdoms burn and something worse than any faction takes power in the vacuum. Resolution should reflect what the two Neutral Evil PCs actually pursued across the campaign — ambition rewarded, corrupted, or consumed.

## Standing rule — hidden info in a single-chat AI-DM context

The `[DM: ...]` bracket convention assumes a human DM reads it privately — in this chat, the user reading these messages **is** the player, so anything in a bracket is immediately visible to them. Never put secret NPC/location knowledge in a bracket before it's earned through a roll — reveal it only in-fiction, only to the extent earned, after the roll. Never expose DC numbers or "passed this tier but not that one" breakdowns either — resolve rolls silently and output only the narrative result. Full detail in the `feedback-ai-dm-hidden-info` memory file.

**Named failure mode, added 2026-08-29**: this also covers scene-setup/quest-prep notes, not just brackets and rolls. Session 7, Millward Crossing — a quest note said "Sefwyn will watch from a distance, uninvolved if anything goes wrong" (DM staging, written ahead of the scene). That line got narrated directly into the read-aloud text as an accomplished fact ("Sefwyn de uzaktan gözlemliyordu") with zero Perception check and zero in-fiction action by the party to notice it — the worst version of this leak, since it wasn't even in a bracket, it was presented as something the party had directly perceived. **The check**: before narrating any hidden observer, off-screen actor, or other DM-only staging detail as something that happened, ask whether the party actually did anything (a check, an active search, the NPC choosing to reveal themselves) that would have surfaced it. If not, the detail stays true in the fiction but unstated to the table — same discipline as a DC number or a failed-tier breakdown.

## Standing rule — write state as it happens, never at session end

**The diagnosis** (2026-08-24, table flagged it): character sheets and `state.md` kept going stale. Three causes, and note that the first one produced errors in *both* directions — XP was written to state.md but not the sheets, while Ilvaneth's conned 18 gp was written to the sheet but not state.md.

1. **Duplication.** HP, gold, XP, and slots lived in two files. Whichever one was in mind got updated; the other silently drifted.
2. **Deferred writes.** Changes were batched to "end of session." Ilvaneth dropping to 3 HP in the goblin ambush and then long-resting existed only in chat. Session 2 never formally closed, so none of it landed.
3. **No verification.** Nothing compared the files, so drift accumulated invisibly.

**The fix is structural, not a reminder:**

- **Character files are the single source of truth** for HP, AC, XP, gold, and slots. `state.md`'s party table is now a *generated block* between `<!-- party-block:start -->` / `<!-- party-block:end -->` markers. Never hand-edit it.
- **Write immediately, with the tool.** Every HP change, gold change, or resource spend goes through `update_character.py` the moment it happens at the table — not later:
  ```
  py .claude/scripts/update_character.py ashen-crown Ilvaneth damage 5
  py .claude/scripts/update_character.py ashen-crown Kriv heal 3
  ```
- **Sync and verify** with the new script:
  ```
  py .claude/scripts/sync_state.py ashen-crown --check   # report drift, exit 1 if found
  py .claude/scripts/sync_state.py ashen-crown --sync    # regenerate from character files
  ```
- **Run `--check` at the start of every session and `--sync` after any character change.** If `--check` reports drift, something was played and never written down — find it before continuing.
- **Sold or discarded items get removed from the character sheet entirely, not struck through and left in place.** Caught 2026-09-01: a sheet's inventory sections had accumulated several `~~item~~ — sold...` entries, cluttering what's supposed to be a clean read of current possessions with history a reader has to visually filter out. Character sheets are a *current-state* document, the same principle already applied to HP/gold/slots — a sold item's history belongs in `campaign-clock.md`'s day-by-day log (already narrative, already dated), not preserved a second time on the sheet. When an item is sold, given away, or destroyed: delete its row/bullet from the character file, and confirm the transaction is captured in that day's `campaign-clock.md` entry if it isn't already.
- Campaign-level facts (location, session #, quests, threads, day count) are still hand-edited in `state.md`, and must be updated **when they change in play**, not at session end. Location and session number were both a full session stale when this was caught.

## Standing rule — roleplay, rewards, and staying out of the player's chair

Full system in **`roleplay-and-xp.md`**. The short version:

- **Enforce mechanics, never personalities.** When an ascension cost is active it's a condition — enforce it like poison. "Kriv reaches for the purse and his hand doesn't open, roll WIS" is correct. "Kriv wouldn't do that" is not.
- **Never withhold XP as punishment.** It penalizes the player rather than the character and teaches safe, boring play. All roleplay XP is additive.
- **Award XP for playing costs, for honest Neutral Evil (including the ugly choices), for the bond, for non-combat resolutions.** Max 3 instances per character per session, tier-scaled. Inspiration in the moment, XP at session end.
- **Never flag roleplay at all.** No pre-declared red lines (decided at the table 2026-08-24 — boundaries emerge through play, not a form). There is no such thing as playing your character wrong: not flagged, not questioned, not even asked about. Characters here are *supposed* to change.
- **The failure mode is skipping the costs.** Powers are fun to give, costs are awkward to enforce, and within three sessions the ascension tracks become a superpower buffet. Check every session: did Kriv's hoard-sense come up? Did Ilvaneth lose a memory? Keep her loss list written at the table.

## Standing rule — advance the ascension tracks on every fragment

**Enforced 2026-08-30 via `py .claude/scripts/ascension_check.py ashen-crown --check`** (wired into `session_state_gate.py`'s `Stop` hook) — computes the correct stage directly from fragment count + level against `ascension-tracks.md`'s own gate table, no longer a memory-dependent check.

**`ascension-tracks.md` is the authority on what the fragments do.** Both PCs transform, one-way, at the same rate, gated by fragment count and level floor. Every time the party gains a fragment: advance both tracks, apply the new power, and — this is the part that gets skipped — **apply the new cost from that session onward.**

The costs are the whole design. Kriv's hoard-sense means he genuinely cannot sell things; play it. Ilvaneth's lost memories go on **a written list at the table** so she can watch it grow. If the powers land and the costs don't, the tracks become a superpower buffet and the campaign loses its spine.

**They both know.** No secrets between them — they swore never to lie and they haven't. The horror is complicity, not concealment: each watches the other become something that will end what they have, and neither asks the other to stop.

Confirmed with the table (2026-08-23): irreversible, no size increase, flight at 17+, both tracks kept balanced.

## Standing rule — both PCs get equal narrative weight

The table caught the campaign building an entire architecture around Ilvaneth's ambition (a dedicated dungeon, a second site answering her question, named treasure, faction leverage, a final confrontation written to address her, two of five endings) while Kriv got three breadcrumbs pointing at a single name. Worse, his goal was written *passively* — "learn who destroyed my house" is waiting for the DM to say a name, not an ambition that generates play.

Fixed in `kriv-thread.md`. **The rule going forward:**

- **Never run a session where one PC's personal thread is live and the other's is dormant.** If Ilvaneth is researching in a library, Kriv has a retainer to find, a rival to lean on, or a claim to press in the same session.
- **Check parity when adding anything.** New site, new NPC, new treasure, new revelation — ask which PC it serves, and if the answer has been the same one twice running, fix it before it ships.
- **Kriv's thread is now active, not passive**: he isn't waiting for a name, he's rebuilding a house. That runs through `power-base.md` directly — the holdings mean something specific to him.
- **The map to the remaining fragments comes through Kriv's inheritance** (the Charge-Roll at Shestendeliath Hold), not Ilvaneth's research. That single decision keeps him structurally central rather than decorative.
- **The oath conflict is the campaign's best drama** — Kriv's inheritance obligates him to keep the fragments apart; Ilvaneth's ambition requires assembling them. Neither is betraying the other. Build the pressure honestly, never force the break.

## Standing rule — run the sandbox, not a corridor

**Read `info/sandbox-index.md` at the start of every session.** Sessions 1-2 ran as one unbranching line — each scene was a direct consequence of the previous one, no content existed that wasn't the main quest, and the Chapter 1 "build a local power base" arc note had nothing attached to it the party could actually grab. The table called this out and it was correct.

The sandbox layer (`sandbox-index.md` and everything it lists) is the fix. Requirements:

- **Three visible options minimum.** Whenever the party finishes anything, they should be able to see at least three things worth doing, with no obviously correct one.
- **Nothing is purely side content.** Every site is also a favor someone wants, a secret someone's keeping, or a resource someone's already using. Every NPC generates work independent of the Crown.
- **The world moves off-screen.** Factions act whether or not the party watches. Before each session, decide what Sarelle, Sefwyn, Kell, Fennick, and the Choir did since last time.
- **Log state back.** Rumors given, encounters rolled, holdings taken, jobs opened/closed — write them into the relevant file, don't leave them in chat scrollback.

## Standing rule — at least one site should owe nothing to the plot

**Why this exists**: session 5 (2026-08-27), the DM pointed out directly that "nothing is purely side content" (the rule immediately above) has a real cost — every single site in the sandbox, checked one by one, turned out to be wired into a faction, an NPC relationship, or a personal arc. Even `the-drowned-mill.md`, the closest thing to a pure treasure site, explicitly has "no neutral way to loot this building" (Vey vs. Kell). There was **no option that was just a dungeon** — go in, fight things, find loot, leave, nothing owed to anyone.

**The fix**: at least one standalone site should exist and be reachable at all times, with zero connection to the Crown, the three factions, or either PC's personal arc. `locations/the-widows-hollow.md` (a green hag's cave system, found via an old misfiled map at Harrowgate's Scriptorium) is the first one, built the same session this was raised. **When it's resolved, build the next one** — this is a standing category, not a one-time addition. Check for it the same way `sandbox-index.md` step 4b now requires.

**This does not replace the interweave rule above** — most sites should still stay interconnected, that's what makes the sandbox feel alive. This is specifically a release valve: one clean option, always available, for a session where the table wants to just go explore something without it mattering to anyone but them.

## Standing rule — dungeons need density, the same way settlements do

**Why this exists**: session 5 (2026-08-27), same session The Widow's Hollow was built and played, the DM called it out directly — too few monsters, one secret room with no in-fiction way to ever find it, rooms that were just empty pass-throughs, and an overall feeling of "moving room to room" rather than being inside a real place. This is the exact same failure shape already caught and fixed for settlements (`dm-notes.md`'s population→location-count table, and the later fix that a headcount alone isn't enough — the *categories* have to actually be checked). A dungeon has the same problem: a room count that looks sufficient on paper can still be mostly empty connective tissue.

**The checklist, going forward, for any new dungeon/site with numbered rooms:**

- **Every room gets *something*, not just the ones with a fight in them.** A found object, a smell, a sound, a sign of what actually lives here (droppings, worn paths, claw marks, leftovers) — even a room with zero mechanical content should read as part of an inhabited place, not a hallway between the rooms that matter.
- **No secret behind a blind percentage roll alone.** Every hidden room/door needs an in-fiction, discoverable clue a player could reasonably act on (a trail, a draft of air, a sound, an object pointing the way) *in addition to* a passive/active check as the fallback for a party that doesn't chase the clue. A DC that nobody could ever have a reason to roll for is not a secret, it's a coin flip nobody knows to make.
- **Aim for real content (a monster, a meaningful find, a choice) in at least half the numbered rooms**, not just 2-3 out of 15+. If a first draft comes in thinner than that, that's the same signal `treasure.md`'s pacing table already tracks for the campaign as a whole — go back and fill in before the party arrives, not after they say it felt empty.
- **A shortcut a clever party finds is allowed to skip content** — don't force an encounter back in because they found a smart bypass. But don't silently delete the skipped content either; leave it written and available for a return trip, and consider letting them glimpse evidence of what they walked past.

**The first fix under this rule**: `locations/the-widows-hollow.md` was revised the same session — every room now carries at least ambient detail, room 6's secret got a findable silk-trail clue (and its blind-roll DC dropped from 15 to 13 as a fallback, not the primary route), and the skipped-content note above was added explicitly rather than left implicit.

**Hook-enforced as of session 13, 2026-09-03** — this written standard alone failed to hold up under combat-pacing pressure three times (Kesh Deeps' ecology rooms, The Maker's Undoing's Rooms 1-6, then the DM repeating the same complaint again at session 13's start before play resumed). `py .claude/scripts/room_description_check.py` (a `Stop` hook) now blocks any turn that starts a new encounter (`combat_status.py --init`) with no sensory/environmental word anywhere in that turn or the one before it. Covers monster-populated rooms, not a pure non-combat room's entry — see `system-register.md`/`bug-log.md` for the honest limitation.

## Standing rule — settlement content scales with stated population, checked before arrival, not after

**Why this exists**: Harrowgate (population 4,000) was built out with 5 locations — a mapmaker, a stable, an inn, an alchemist, a smith — and played that way for a full session before the DM caught that it read like a village, not a market town. The fix applied *after* being caught (session 3) added 5 more locations. That's a real fix, but it was reactive, and the campaign has bigger settlements coming: Karsgate (5 districts + undercity), Emberhold (the Chapter 2 capital). The same gap will recur at a larger scale unless it's checked *before* the party arrives, not after they notice.

**Minimum location count by stated population** — check `gazetteer.md`'s number for the settlement, then check its location file (if one exists) actually has this many distinct locations before the party arrives:

| Population | Tier | Minimum distinct locations |
|-----------|------|----------------------------|
| Under 500 | Hamlet/village | 3-4 (per the existing rule below — one room does not equal a village) |
| 500-3,000 | Town | 6-8 (inn, market/trading post, **a religious site**, smith, one authority figure's seat, one specialty shop) |
| 3,000-10,000 | Market town / small city | 10-15, and consider splitting by district or function if the file is getting long — **at least one religious site per district that plausibly has one** |
| 10,000+ | City / capital | 15+, organized by district — do not attempt this as a single flat list; give each district its own subsection or its own file. **Multiple religious sites** — a city this size has more than one faith/faction present |

**A religious site is not optional — caught missing entirely, 2026-08-26.** This is a funerary-themed campaign with three religious/quasi-religious factions in open conflict (the Ashlord Concordat's ash-magic, the Cinder Choir's death cult, the orthodox Ember Crown temple) plus local folk practice (Cair Dunnow's own funeral rites). A settlement with no shrine, temple, or altar at all is the exception requiring a specific in-fiction reason (actively suppressed, too new, too poor even for a shrine), not the default. Match the site to who actually has influence there: a border town like Gallowmere gets something informal and unclaimed (a communal shrine, no dominant sect yet — a power vacuum, not empty ground); a real town like Harrowgate gets an actual temple with a named cleric; a city gets more than one, representing different factions or none.

**The trigger**: this is now part of session prep, the same way `system-register.md`'s ❌ items are checked at session start (`sandbox-index.md` step 0). Before a session where the party is heading toward a named settlement, check its location file's actual location count against the table above. If it's under-built for its stated population, flesh it out *then* — before the arrival scene, not during it and not after a correction. **Karsgate is the next one this will matter for** — it's explicitly 5 districts in `gazetteer.md` and currently has none of them detailed.

**Hitting the number is not the same as passing the check — verify the named categories, not just the count.** Caught 2026-08-27: Gallowmere itself (the party's own home base, population ~1,200, Town tier) had 9 distinct locations — comfortably clearing the 6-8 minimum — but had never actually had an inn or a smith, two of the six categories the Town-tier row explicitly names. The 2026-08-26 sweep that checked Gallowmere against this table only counted total locations and marked it fine; it never checked the parenthetical category list item by item. Thornwick, a smaller village one tier down with no category requirement at all, had both. Going forward, checking a settlement against this table means **listing the tier's named categories and finding each one by name in the location file** — a headcount alone is not sufficient evidence the settlement is actually built out. Fixed: added The Salt Lantern (inn, run by Genna Ostler) and Coalsmoke Forge (smith, run by Torv Adder) to `locations/gallowmere.md`. Swept the other established settlements at this pass: Sallow Ford (~400, Hamlet/village tier, no category list applies) already has an inn (The Long Wait) and meets its simpler 3-4 count; Thornwick and Harrowgate already had all six Town-tier categories present. No further settlement needed fixing this pass.

**This is a template for the fix, not a one-time patch on Harrowgate.** Apply the same table to every settlement the party reaches for the first time, regardless of how thin or complete it currently looks.

## Standing rule — settlements need more than one location

Caught compressing an entire 35-40 household village (Thornwick) into a single catch-all tavern that handled lodging, drinking, gambling, and trading all at once — a shortcut, not a framework limitation. Going forward, when introducing any settlement of meaningful size, sketch out a handful of distinct locations (tavern/inn, a trading post or general store, a smithy, a shrine/temple, etc. as fits the place) and write them to `campaigns/ashen-crown/info/locations/[name].md` rather than routing everything through one room by default. Not every location needs to be detailed in play immediately — just needs to plausibly exist so the party isn't stuck in a "one room does everything" village.

## Standing rule — richer environment description

The user flagged that scene/location narration has been too sparse — functional but thin on the world around the party (sensory detail, atmosphere, visible signs of the setting's state, small worldbuilding texture). Going forward, location and arrival narration should lean fuller: what it looks/sounds/smells like, what's visibly happening around the party, concrete details tying back to the campaign's state (succession war, factions, local conditions) — not just "you arrive, X is here, what do you do." Keep it to the read-aloud layer's normal length guidance (2-5 sentences is a floor here, not a ceiling, when arriving somewhere new) rather than clipped one-liners.

## Standing rule — combat damage rolls

Never invent or assume damage numbers on the DM's behalf for a player character's attack, including crits. When a player's attack roll hits, always explicitly ask them to roll and report the damage dice themselves (including doubled dice on a crit) before narrating the result — the same way attack rolls and skill checks work at this table. Only resolve/narrate the outcome after the player reports the actual damage roll. This mirrors the existing house rule that players always report raw dice and the DM adds modifiers/interprets — it applies to damage too, not just d20 checks.

**Incident**: broke this rule in the session 2 goblin ambush — narrated Goblin B's death from a critical Fire Bolt without asking for the 2d10 damage roll first. Corrected mid-combat (2026-08-23); the damage roll was requested retroactively and the outcome adjusted to match.

## Standing rule — run the intake check on any new capability

**`.claude/rules/new-capabilities.md`** — the first time a new spell, class feature, magic item, or ascension stage is used in play, run the six questions: noise/light, witnesses, physical evidence, **is it identifying**, does a faction care, does it touch the ascension. Once per capability, not per casting. Say the answers in the `[DM: …]` layer and record the non-trivial ones so the second use matches the first.

**The live issue for this party**: the Concordat holds descriptions of "an elf and a dragonborn" since day ~5. Kriv's breath weapon in front of survivors is a signed confession, and his ascension only makes that worse. Disguise Self is not a utility spell for this party — it is infrastructure.

## Standing rule — never improvise a missing system silently

Full protocol in **`.claude/rules/missing-systems.md`**, status board in **`info/system-register.md`**.

This project is a production system under active development, aimed at commercial-grade quality (see `CLAUDE.md`'s "Producer's Vision" section) — not a hobby prototype. When play needs a system, template, or rule that doesn't exist: **stop, say so plainly, build it into the right file, log it in the register, then continue.** Never invent one on the spot and keep the scene moving — that hides the gap, produces a different answer next time, and leaves the table unable to tell which systems are real.

The line: *would this need to behave the same way next time?* If yes, it's a system — stop and write it. If it's a one-off ruling, just rule it. Anything built in a hurry gets marked `provisional`.

**Highest-priority known gap right now**: companion/sidekick rules — needed the moment the party recruits anyone.

## Standing rule — default to inline, not to agents

The upstream framework mandates sub-agent delegation in three separate places, and each one relays messages turn-by-turn. Confirmed in play across three scenes: **60-100 seconds per exchange**, which makes normal play impossible. All three are overridden in `CLAUDE.md`:

| Framework mandate | Override | Why it's safe |
|---|---|---|
| NPC dialogue → `conversation-tracker` | Voice inline | The framework's own skill file says the agent "cannot access the raw conversation and will hallucinate content" |
| Combat → `combat-tracker`, relay every turn | Run inline with `combat_status.py` | The agent's job is state tracking; the script does it instantly and deterministically |
| Rules → `srd-lookup` agent | Call `srd_lookup.py` directly | The agent's entire job is running that script. It returns in a fraction of a second |

**Agents are still correct for genuine one-shot batch work**: `/dm-start-session`, `/dm-end-session`, `/dm-index-campaign`, `story-teller`. The test is simple — if the work happens turn-by-turn or line-by-line, it belongs inline. **If you're about to send a third message to the same agent, it should not have been an agent.**

## Standing rule — never run conversation-tracker live

The framework's default (in `CLAUDE.md`, unmodified from upstream) spawns `conversation-tracker` for any named NPC and keeps it alive, relaying every line to it. Tested in play across two scenes: **every exchange costs a full agent round-trip, 60-100 seconds.** Corran's shop and Hesta Bram's interview both became unplayable at that pace.

**Overridden in `CLAUDE.md` — voice all NPCs inline, in the main context.**

The decisive argument isn't only speed. The framework's own `dm-conversation-log` skill warns: *"Do NOT delegate to the conversation-tracker agent — it cannot access the raw conversation and will hallucinate content."* The live agent works second-hand from relayed messages; the main context holds the actual dialogue. **Inline is both faster and more accurate.**

**What inline play must remember to do** — the jobs the agent was handling:
1. Note disposition shifts in the `[DM: …]` layer as they happen
2. Run `/dm-conversation-log [campaign] [npc-slug]` once when a substantive conversation ends — one call, not one per line
3. Update `party/relationships.md` when a disposition actually moves
4. Use `/dm-checkpoint-log [campaign]` if context gets heavy on a long session — this is the real answer to the context-budget concern the agent existed for

Transactional exchanges need no log.

## Standing rule — cinematic openings for big moments only

Full guidance in **`.claude/rules/cinematic-moments.md`**.

Four triggers, and only these: **a new campaign or arc opens · first arrival at any named location (expanded 2026-08-30 from "kingdom/city/major seat of power" to every site with a `locations/*.md` file, per direct DM instruction) · a boss fight begins · a major quest closes.** Everything else — corridors and rooms *within* an already-entered location, minor NPCs, regular combat, returning somewhere known — uses normal two-layer narration.

The move is wide → close in 4-8 sentences: the world, then the human layer, then the characters right now. **It must land on the PCs.** Optional one-or-two-sentence flashback before the wide shot for arc openings and major closings.

Bosses get a prepared character layer: entrance line, voice, what they want, a bloodied line, a line for when a PC drops, and last words. One to three sentences each, spoken *between* rounds rather than instead of acting, and never explaining the plot.

**The limit is the rule.** Used every scene this stops working. A handful of times per arc.

## Standing rule — long rest recovery is a script gate, not a memory

**Incident (2026-08-25)**: On the Thornwick→Gallowmere leg, the party established a nightly routine (Ilvaneth Trance + watch, daily Mage Armor). Three separate times, HP/spell slots/hit dice/features were never actually refreshed despite nights passing in the narration — each time caught only because the DM noticed the numbers didn't add up. A written reminder ("remember to apply long rest") failed three times in one session. It is not the fix.

**The fix is `py .claude/scripts/rest_check.py ashen-crown --check`** — a hard gate, not a prompt to remember something:

- It tracks the in-fiction day a long rest was last actually applied (`**Last Long Rest**: Day N` in `state.md`) against the current day.
- **Run `--check` any time the in-fiction day is about to advance**, or right before resolving a combat or scene after narrating that a night has passed. It exits with an error (DRIFT) if a rest is owed — this is not optional to act on, the same way `sync_state.py --check` failing is not optional to ignore.
- If it reports DRIFT, run `py .claude/scripts/rest_check.py ashen-crown --apply` before continuing the scene. It fully restores HP, hit dice, spell slots, and features for every PC in one call and re-marks the day as rested. Do not narrate around a DRIFT result.
- A travel-encounter roll that costs a night's rest (combat, no safe camp) means don't advance the Last Long Rest day for that night — the gate is meant to catch *forgotten* rests, not force one that didn't happen in the fiction.

## Standing rule — NPCs act on their own self-interest, never on the plot's convenience

Full system in **`.claude/rules/npc-consistency.md`**. Short version: before narrating any NPC offer, concession, or favor, it must trace back to that NPC's own stated self-interest — never to what's convenient for the scene. Every NPC file carries two living sections ("Current knowledge & stance," "Decision logic") that get updated the instant that NPC learns something new in play, not just written once at creation and left static. Built 2026-08-27 after Orell Tain was played offering free help with no basis in his file.

## Standing rule — every NPC gets a visible physical description, every time they appear

Caught 2026-08-26: Sefwyn Marrow's first face-to-face scene skipped their established look (`npcs/sefwyn-marrow.md`'s "Surface" section — lean, grey-clothed, unremarkable on purpose) and only narrated behavior. `dm-narration-format` skill requires 1-3 sentences on NPC entrance but doesn't spell out *what* those sentences must contain, or require refreshing the image on later reappearances.

**Going forward, any NPC's first appearance in a scene (first-ever meeting, or a returning NPC's first appearance in *this* scene) gets a short read-aloud description covering:**
- **Race/species** (human, elf, orc, etc.)
- **Sex/gender**
- **Approximate age** (or age-equivalent: "weathered," "barely past childhood")
- **2-3 distinguishing physical details** — build, clothing, a scar, a mannerism, whatever makes them a person and not a name

**New NPCs get a file, not just a description.** Any NPC introduced with a name and enough substance to matter again (not a one-line shopkeeper) gets a proper file in `campaigns/ashen-crown/info/npcs/[name].md` the same session they're introduced — role, disposition, motive, connections, and a suggested SRD stat block if they might ever come to blows. Don't let a named NPC exist only as a paragraph inside another file (`faction-subplots.md`, an encounter file, etc.) once they've actually appeared in play — that's prep, not a record. Missed once already: Magus Orell Tain was named and given real weight in session 3 before anyone noticed he only existed as a paragraph in `faction-subplots.md`.

**The physical-description half applies to unnamed/transactional NPCs too — caught 2026-08-29.** Session 7: a travel-encounter merchant convoy got full dialogue and a rumor exchange with zero physical description at all, and Tain's own aide got only "a young aide" with no race chosen (violating the race-variety rule above too). Neither needed a file (the file requirement's "not a one-line shopkeeper" carve-out correctly excludes them) — but the physical-description requirement has no such carve-out in its own text, and both got skipped anyway because "background" NPCs read as low-stakes enough to shortcut. **The description doesn't need a file behind it** — a merchant or an aide gets the same 1-2 sentence read-aloud treatment (race, sex/gender, approximate age, a physical detail or two) as anyone else, on the spot, with no separate NPC file required.

This is 1-2 sentences, not a paragraph — stays inside the existing read-aloud length budget. Pull from the NPC's file if one exists (most do, under "Surface" or similar); invent consistent details on the spot if not, and record them in the NPC's file afterward so the next appearance matches. Don't repeat the full description on every line of dialogue in the same scene — once per scene, at the entrance, is enough; a brief physical beat (a gesture, an expression) is fine as color afterward but doesn't need to be the full description again.

## Standing rule — combat XP is now a hard gate, not memory

**Updated 2026-08-30**: the combat-XP half of this rule (below) is no longer memory-dependent. `combat_status.py --init` now sets `xp_awarded: false` on the encounter; `combat_xp_check.py` (wired into `session_state_gate.py`'s `Stop` hook) flags a DRIFT if every enemy is dead and that flag is still false. Run `py .claude/scripts/combat_status.py ashen-crown --xp-awarded` the same turn `xp_add` is called for every PC. The rest-application half (long rest via `rest_check.py`, short rest still uncovered) is unchanged — see below.

## Standing rule — combat XP and rest application are checklist items, not memory

**Incident (session 3, 2026-08-26)**: combat XP was forgotten twice in one session (the 12-person Concordat convoy, the hell hound) and only caught because the DM asked "why isn't XP included?" both times. Long-rest application after multi-night travel was also missed and only caught by `rest_check.py`'s drift detection, not proactively. This is the same failure shape the rest-tracking and RP-XP-cap gates were built to prevent — a reminder that only works if remembered is not a fix.

**Confirmed still failing after the first fix (same session, twice more)**: writing "do this the instant X happens" was not enough — the failure mode is that narration gets written first (it's the fun part) and the mechanical step becomes a follow-up that's easy to drop once the next player message arrives. A rule phrased as "do the check after the trigger" still leaves room to skip the check.

**The actual fix — reverse the order, don't just speed up the follow-up:**

- **Before writing any prose that resolves a fight's end** (last enemy dead/fled/surrendered), the XP math and `xp_add` calls happen first, as the tool calls that precede the response, not after it. The response is not allowed to contain victory narration until the XP calls are already in it.
- **Before writing any prose that resolves a night passing** ("kamp kuruyoruz", "sabah oluyor," any full-rest transition), `rest_check.py --check` (and `--apply` if it reports DRIFT) happens first, in the same tool-call batch as whatever else is being resolved that turn — not narrated now and checked "right after."
- **Before writing any prose that resolves a short rest**, the actual `update_character.py <name> features_restore_all` calls (Second Wind, Action Surge, Superiority Dice, Breath Weapon, Arcane Recovery — whichever apply) happen first, for every PC resting, in the same batch. **Confirmed failing in play (session 5, 2026-08-27, twice in one fight)**: a short rest was narrated as restoring Kriv's Action Surge and Superiority Dice, and separately Ilvaneth's Arcane Recovery, but neither script call was actually made — the character files kept the pre-rest values, and both mismatches only surfaced when the players tried to use the "restored" resource and the file disagreed. Unlike long rests, short rests have no `rest_check.py`-style hook gate (there's no day-counter to check drift against), so this one depends entirely on the tool call actually being made in the same turn as the narration — there is no backstop here besides catching it live.
- **Before writing any prose that resolves a gold/XP change of any kind**, `sync_state.py --sync` runs in the same batch.
- **Before writing arrival narration for a settlement/kingdom/seat of power**, check `state.md`'s "Visited locations" list first (see `cinematic-moments.md`). **Order matters, not just the check** (caught 2026-09-02, `bug-log.md`, `workflow-skip` — The Maker's Undoing's real cinematic was skipped entirely): edit `Current Location` to name the new site *before* writing the arrival prose, in the same turn — the `PreToolUse` hook that enforces this keys off that exact edit, and narrating the arrival first, with the state.md edit only catching up afterward (or in a later turn), lets the trigger moment slip past unnoticed. The tool call is the precondition for the narration, same principle as the XP/rest-application rule below.

The shared principle: **the tool call is a precondition for the narration, not a cleanup task after it.** This doesn't make the failure mode impossible — there's no execution hook forcing any of this, and it depends on actually re-reading this section before generating the response — but it removes the specific trap of "the fun sentence gets written and the bookkeeping never catches up." The DM catching a miss and asking remains the real backstop; it has worked every time it's been used and should keep being used without hesitation.

## Standing rule — never roll a player's dice, never invent your own

Violated in the first combat, both halves at once: Claude rolled Thunderwave's 2d8 damage (Ilvaneth's roll to make), and stated three Constitution saves for the deserters *after* the `roll.py` call had failed — so those numbers were never rolled at all, just written.

**Full ownership table is in `CLAUDE.md`.** The short version:

- **Player rolls** — PC attacks, **damage including spell damage**, saves, checks, death saves, hit dice. Ask and *wait*. Never roll these to keep the scene moving; that is the DM playing the character.
- **Claude's rolls** — enemy attacks, enemy saves, enemy damage, travel and random tables. Always via `roll.py`.

**The hard rule: never state a die result that did not come from either a player reporting it or visible `roll.py` output.** If a script call fails, fix it and retry — do not substitute a plausible number. An invented number is not a roll no matter how reasonable it looks.

**Repeat incident, session 4 (2026-08-26)**: Ilvaneth's Burning Hands damage was rolled by Claude via `roll.py` instead of the player — the identical failure to the Thunderwave incident above, meaning this prose table alone had already failed once and failed again. `roll.py` now hard-requires `--owner enemy|dm-table|npc` on every plain dice call and refuses to run without it, per `bug-protocol.md`'s escalation ladder (a written rule that's already failed once doesn't get fixed by rewriting it again). This still depends on Claude labeling honestly, but makes the decision an explicit, visible one on every single roll instead of a silent default.

**The flag does not stop a dishonest label — session 6 proved it, twice (2026-08-27, Skree's Warren and the Millward Crossing convoy ambush).** Both times, Ilvaneth's Burning Hands damage was rolled via `roll.py --owner dm-table` — the flag was present and filled in, and still wrong, because "dm-table" is not an honest label for a PC's own spell damage. **The specific trap: when one spell hits several enemies at once, resolving "one roll for the whole group" starts to feel like a DM-side table effect instead of what it is — the casting player's damage roll, exactly as much as a single-target Fire Bolt.** Hitting multiple targets with one PC spell does not change whose roll the damage is. Burning Hands, Thunderwave, Scorching Ray's three rays, any AoE — if a player character cast it, the player rolls the damage, every time, regardless of how many creatures are in the area. `--owner dm-table` is for travel tables and genuine DM-side environmental effects only, never for a spell a PC chose to cast.

## Standing rule — player-controlled summons/undead are the player's own unit, full stop

**Confirmed by the DM, Session 14 (2026-09-03).** Any creature raised or controlled by a player through a spell or effect (Animate Dead, and anything of the same shape going forward) is that player's own unit, exactly like their PC — **the player rolls all of its dice** (attacks, saves, damage), never Claude, and **it shares the controlling player's initiative** rather than getting a separate roll of its own.

This generalizes what already happened informally this session (Ilvaneth's zombie thrall, Perrine Oskold, was ruled to share her initiative) into a standing rule for every future summon.

**Mechanical support**: `combat_status.py`'s `--update` patch now supports `{"add_combatant": {...}}` to add a summon mid-combat with `"type": "ally"` and `"initiative_source": "<PC name>"` — it copies that PC's current initiative directly rather than rolling a fresh one, and displays as `(Ally)` rather than the misleading `(Enemy)` label the tracker had no other way to express before this fix (see `bug-log.md`, 2026-09-03 — Ilvaneth's zombie thrall this session had to be hand-edited into `combat_state.json` because no add-combatant path existed at all). A dead ally is skipped by `advance_turn` the same way a dead enemy is (no death saves), and `{"name": "...", "type": "pc|enemy|ally"}` can retype an existing combatant if needed.

**What stays a judgment call**: whether a given effect actually counts as "player-controlled" (Animate Dead obviously does; a Conjure Animals-summoned beast under standard rules technically also does; a charmed/dominated enemy does NOT — that's still an enemy-turned-ally in fiction, not a player's own raised unit) — that distinction isn't mechanically diffable and stays a DM call at the point a new summon type shows up.

**Stat bonuses from the caster's own class features, added 2026-09-04**: `add_combatant` fixed *turn tracking* for summons; it never touched the separate problem of a caster's own feature (Ilvaneth's Undead Thralls — bonus HP equal to Wizard level, proficiency bonus added to weapon damage) modifying the summon's *base stats*, which got missed on the thralls' first real combat use (`bug-log.md`, 2026-09-04, `workflow-skip`). Before setting a summon's HP/damage for `add_combatant` or any ad-hoc combat resolution, run:
```
py .claude/scripts/summon_stat_check.py <campaign> <caster> --base-hp <N> --base-dmg "<XdY+Z>" --type <creature-type>
```
It reads the caster's own Level/Proficiency Bonus and a small, extensible table of known summon-boosting features, and prints the adjusted HP/damage to actually use. Extend `KNOWN_SUMMON_EFFECTS` in that script the next time a different caster's feature does something similar.

## Standing rule — roll dice for real

There is no built-in randomness in this chat, so "I rolled a 7" has previously meant *the DM picked 7*. The table caught this in session 2. **`roll.py` is the fix** — it uses `random.SystemRandom` and parses the encounter tables straight out of `travel-encounters.md`:

```
py .claude/scripts/roll.py --travel ashen-crown thornlands 5   # travel table, 5 nights
py .claude/scripts/roll.py --tables ashen-crown                # list tables
py .claude/scripts/roll.py 1d20+5                              # plain dice
py .claude/scripts/roll.py 4d6 --drop-lowest                   # stat rolls
```

Any roll the DM makes — travel encounters, monster attacks, saves, random tables — goes through this. Never invent a number and present it as a roll. Player-side rolls stay with the players.

## Standing rule — travel encounters

**Enforced 2026-08-30 via `py .claude/scripts/travel_roll_check.py ashen-crown`** (Stop hook) — flags a turn narrating "Gece 1"/"Night 1" of a journey with no `roll.py ... --travel` call in that same turn.

Whenever the party travels overland between locations for more than a few hours (a day or more of road travel), roll on the relevant table in `campaigns/ashen-crown/info/travel-encounters.md` for each day/night of the journey **before** narrating what happens — don't just invent an event freehand. Log every roll and its outcome in that file's roll log so nothing gets re-rolled, forgotten, or retconned. If no table exists yet for the route being traveled, create one there first (fits the region/danger level), then roll on it.

# Kriv's Thread — House Shestendeliath

**Why this file exists**: Ilvaneth's ambition got an entire architecture — a dedicated dungeon, a second site answering her question, named treasure, faction leverage, a final confrontation written to address her directly, and two of the five endings. Kriv got three breadcrumbs pointing at one name. That was a design failure, and worse, his goal as written was *passive*: "find out who destroyed my house" is a mystery to wait for, not an ambition to pursue.

This is the fix. Kriv's thread now runs in parallel, with beats in both chapters, its own sites, its own antagonists, and its own thematic question.

---

## The truth about House Shestendeliath

**They were Ash-Wardens.** Dragonborn oath-nobility, sworn shields of the empire — and House Shestendeliath held a hereditary office: guardianship of **one fragment of the Ashen Crown**. Not a symbolic honour. An actual, binding, generations-deep charge, sworn in draconic oath-magic, to keep one piece of the relic from ever rejoining the others.

**That is why they were destroyed.** Not court intrigue over land or titles. Someone wanted the fragment, and the only way to it was through the house sworn to guard it.

## The three layers of guilt

Kriv's question doesn't have one answer. It has three, and they surface at different depths.

### Layer 1 — the swords *(Chapter 1, discoverable ~level 5-8)*
**The Ironclad Compact** brokered and executed it. They sold the intelligence and provided the men. To them it was a contract; Kriv's family was a line item. **Coren Ashvale** knows, and will trade the name — see `npcs/coren-ashvale.md`.

### Layer 2 — the blood *(Chapter 1 climax for this thread, level 6-10)*
**The house was betrayed from inside.** Kriv's uncle, **Vharkoss Shestendeliath**, sold the Warden rotation and the fragment's location in exchange for his own survival and elevation. He is alive, prosperous, and living under another name in Karsgate. See `npcs/vharkoss-shestendeliath.md`.

### Layer 3 — the hand *(Chapter 2)*
**The Ashlord Concordat commissioned it** — Sarelle Duskbourne's predecessor, and Sarelle inherited the file. She knows exactly whose house she is standing on. She will use it: offering Kriv restoration by imperial decree is an extremely effective inducement when you are the reason he needs it. See `npcs/sarelle-duskbourne.md`.

## ★ The revelation — the fragment they already hold

**The Gallowmere ossuary fragment is the Shestendeliath fragment.**

When the house fell, a surviving retainer fled south with two things: the fragment, and the hatchling heir. They hid the fragment in a border-town ossuary behind a Warden's containment ward, left the child at an orphanage in the same town, and did not survive long enough to tell anyone.

This reframes everything already played:
- **Kriv was in Gallowmere because the fragment was in Gallowmere.** The orphanage wasn't arbitrary — the same person put them both there.
- Mother Vey's job put Kriv in front of his own inheritance without either of them knowing.
- **The dormant guardian is an Ash-Warden.** Possibly the retainer themself, bound by the oath rather than released by death. It was sworn to prevent any piece being taken *unearned* — and Kriv, the last legitimate Shestendeliath, is the one person alive who could have simply claimed it.

They snuck past his family's sworn servant. It is still down there, still on duty, still waiting.

**How to reveal**: not as an announcement. Through Shestendeliath Hold's records, through Vharkoss, through the guardian itself if the party returns. Let Kriv assemble it.

**Table note**: this is strong but adjustable. If the table would rather the ossuary fragment be unrelated, move the Shestendeliath fragment to Shestendeliath Hold and keep everything else.

## Kriv's thematic question

Ilvaneth asks: *can I stop being nothing?*

Kriv's carries equal weight:

> **Is legitimacy real — or is it only ever whoever is holding the sword?**

He was born into a house whose entire worth was its oath and its name, and watched both count for nothing against money and betrayal. The Ashen Crown is a machine for manufacturing legitimacy. He can use it to have his name restored *properly*, by decree, by right — or he can conclude that the whole apparatus is a lie and simply become the largest thing in the world.

**That is this campaign's central question, and Kriv is the character asking it.**

## ★ The real ambition — the blood

Restoring the house is the version he can say out loud. Underneath it is something he'd only admit to one person:

> *I never want to be a thing that can be broken into in the night again. Dragons are not that.*

**House Shestendeliath descends from an actual dragon**, and the blood is dormant, and it can wake. This isn't a tangent from the house plot — **it is the house's actual secret.** The Ash-Warden houses were chosen for dragon blood on purpose: if you need something guarded for four hundred years, you pick a bloodline with long memory, hoarding instinct, and territorial permanence.

The full mechanical track — stages, powers, and costs — is in **`ascension-tracks.md`**, held alongside Ilvaneth's so the two never drift apart.

### What this does to the oath conflict

The Ash-Warden charge obligates his line to keep the fragments **apart**.
His waking blood advances only as fragments are **gathered**.

So the conflict is no longer just Kriv-versus-Ilvaneth. **It is inside him.** His inheritance says scatter them; his blood says bring them closer; and he wants what the blood is offering more than he wants to be a good Warden.

That makes him the hungry one rather than the dutiful one — which is the correct character for Neutral Evil, and a far better engine than "man carrying an inherited obligation."

---

## The Shestendeliath oath — a mechanical asset

Dragonborn oath-magic is binding across generations. As the last legitimate heir, Kriv can **invoke** it:

- **Command the ossuary guardian** — and any other surviving Warden construct. It must obey the house's heir.
- **Compel a truthful accounting** from any oath-sworn Shestendeliath retainer, however long ago they broke faith. This works on Vharkoss and he cannot refuse it.
- **The cost**: an oath binds both ways. Invoking it re-binds Kriv to the Warden charge — the duty to keep the fragments *apart*. Which is the opposite of what Ilvaneth needs.

**This is the wedge.** Not a betrayal — a genuine, structural conflict between two people who swore never to betray each other. Kriv's inheritance obligates him to prevent exactly what Ilvaneth's ambition requires.

Introduce it around level 5-6. Let it sit. Do not resolve it early.

## A fourth layer, surfaced building `factions/cinder-choir.md` (session 7)

The Choir's own correspondence (already read by the party, day 33) shows active interest in **Ash-Warden bloodlines** specifically — very plausibly because dragon-blooded lines read as unusually resistant to a "true" death, making them ideal candidates for the Choir's died-and-returned doctrine. **Kriv doesn't know the Choir is already interested in bloodlines like his.** If this connects, it elevates him from an incidental Crown-bearer to a specific object of Choir attention, independent of anything Ilvaneth does or becomes — a genuine fourth thread for his own arc, not borrowed from hers. Don't force the connection; seed it the way Sister Mave's shrine lead was seeded, and let the party find it.

## Running this well

- **Never let a session be about Ilvaneth's goal without Kriv's thread also having a live thing.** If she's in a library, he should have a name to chase, a retainer to find, a rival to lean on.
- **Kriv's goal is active now**: he isn't waiting for a name, he's rebuilding a house. That means recruiting retainers, reclaiming property, forcing recognition — which uses the `power-base.md` machinery directly. The holdings mean something *specific* to him. **Now concrete as of session 10, day 51**: see `info/shestendeliath-restoration.md` for the actual three-phase plan (physical repairs, living staff/lands, legal recognition) Kethrax laid out when asked.
- **Sethra and Vharkoss are living antagonists**, not mysteries. He can hunt them at his own pace.
- **The oath conflict is the campaign's best drama.** Two people who swore never to betray each other, whose goals are structurally incompatible. Build pressure honestly; don't force the break.

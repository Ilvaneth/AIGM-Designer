# Shestendeliath Hold — Restoration Plan

**Why this file exists**: session 10, day 51 — Kriv told Ser Kethrax the house would rise again, and asked for a concrete plan. This is that plan: three real phases, grounded in what Kethrax actually knows and what's already established elsewhere in the campaign (Tain's attainder research, the region's population, `holdings.md`'s discipline that a `Held` number must trace to something that actually happened in play).

**Status**: **Phase 1 COMPLETE, fully paid** (fired day 74, session 17, at the early 2-week edge of the estimate) — the party hired Ostrig Kelmar, a Harrowgate master mason/foreman (`npcs/ostrig-kelmar.md`), Persuasion 13 (Kriv, Ilvaneth's help). Standard terms: 300 gp total, 150 gp deposit paid day 60, **150 gp completion payment already left with Roskel** (Kriv's own standing instruction before departing the Hold) — Roskel pays Kelmar directly on completion, no action needed from the party in Gallowmere. Delivered via Sending Stone as a status update, not a request for payment. Phase 2 (Greyholt, living staff) is the next real step. See `info/holdings.md`.

---

## Phase 1 — Physical repairs

**The problem, per Kethrax**: the great hall's roof burned through fifteen years ago and was never patched — rain has been getting into the upper floor ever since. The gate, walls, and lower structure are sound; the fire damage is contained to the hall and the rooms above it.

**What it needs**: a real mason and carpenter, materials hauled in from Harrowgate (a day's ride), and enough hands to make it fast rather than slow. This isn't a job the bound garrison can do — Kethrax was direct about that.

**Estimated cost**: ~300 gp, 2-3 weeks, if craftsmen are hired out of Harrowgate and materials bought there. Cheaper and slower if the party instead waits to recruit the phase 2 workforce first and has them do it themselves — a real tradeoff, not a fixed path.

**Risk while ongoing**: Low — a work crew at an isolated, off-road ruin draws little attention on its own. That changes once Phase 2 puts people living there full-time.

---

## Phase 2 — Living staff and lands

**The problem, per Kethrax**: the bound retainers can guard, but they can't farm, cook, or repair anything with their hands. The house needs living people back on its land — and Kethrax doesn't know whether any of the families who once worked these lands are still nearby, fifteen years on.

**Started, day 62-63, session 14 — via an unplanned route.** Ilvaneth, independently pursuing Dunnel Ashe's cure in Ostwick, recruited both Maren and Dunnel Ashe as the Hold's first two living staff — not through Greyholt at all, but as the price of curing Dunnel's lycanthropy (Remove Curse). Both depart Ostwick day 63, bound to serve. This is genuinely the first real headcount for Phase 2, though it's two people, not a real workforce — Greyholt itself is still unapproached, and the question of whether *that* community would come back remains completely open.

**What it still needs**: Greyholt itself — whether any of the old tenant families are still there, and whether they'd actually trust a Shestendeliath heir enough to come back, is unresolved. Fear and old habit don't undo in one conversation, and two indentured outsiders from Ostwick don't answer that question.

**A second, parallel recruitment route opened day 74, session 17** — separate from Greyholt entirely. Kriv found Alis Wend (the refugee family he and Ilvaneth conned in session 2) at the Gallowmere refugee camp and offered her and her children permanent security in exchange for service — she accepted, not knowing his real name or house yet. She's now gathering other camp families/individuals on his behalf: the **Bertrand family** (dwarves, farming background), **Vesper** (a young tiefling, alone, reliable), and **Corrik** (an old human carpenter). 20 gp given for preparations; Kriv will send word when it's time to travel. This is a real headcount candidate for Phase 2 — refugee-camp recruits, not Greyholt's old tenant families, but living staff all the same.

**How this plays out**: a scene at Greyholt, not a gold cost — Persuasion, a demonstrated show of good faith (the garrison no longer being a threat is the party's strongest card), or simply patience and repeated contact. If it succeeds, ongoing wages become a real Upkeep line once staff are actually in place — that number gets set from what's actually negotiated, not estimated here in advance. The Ashes' own terms (service in exchange for safety/cure, not wages) are a separate arrangement, not a template for Greyholt's free tenant families.

**Risk**: Medium — fifteen years of a haunted-ruin reputation doesn't reverse cleanly. A failed first approach could harden Greyholt's fear rather than easing it, and Vharkoss (still at large, still connected to whoever fast-tracked the attainder) has a direct interest in the house *not* successfully re-establishing itself.

**Day 81, session 17 — Phase 2 restarted with real manpower.** The relocated Gallowmere garrison (27 soldiers) and Alis Wend's civilian group (the Bertrand family — dwarf farmers, Vesper, Corrik the carpenter) changed the equation: Phase 2 no longer waits on Greyholt alone. Roskel's plan, approved by Kriv: (1) the Bertrand family plus assigned soldiers begin working the Hold's own land immediately; (2) Corrik plus soldiers with construction skill begin building real housing, with temporary tents in the meantime for the overflow population; (3) Greyholt itself is set aside as a separate approach — **Kriv will personally handle it when the time is right**, using the Hold's now-real, visible restoration (garrison, household, activity) as proof it's no longer a haunted ruin, rather than rushing a first contact.

## Phase 3 — Legal recognition

**The problem**: repairing stone doesn't undo an attainder. House Shestendeliath was formally stripped of its charge, and per Kethrax, "nobody has to recognize you as its lord unless someone with the authority makes it official."

**This is not solvable with gold.** It runs through the same thread already in motion: Magus Orell Tain's ongoing research into who signed off on the attainder (`campaign-clock.md`'s "Tain's attainder research," fires ~day 61). Whatever he finds — a name, a dead end, or a warning — is what determines whether Phase 3 becomes a political fight, a legal petition, or something that has to be forced rather than argued.

**No cost estimate given** — this phase's shape depends entirely on what Tain's research turns up. Revisit this section once that clock fires.

**Day 65, session 15**: Kriv named himself Lord of Shestendeliath Hold in front of Roskel and the newly-arrived Ashe siblings, and named Roskel the highest authority present in his own absence. **This is an internal household declaration, not Phase 3's legal recognition** — it settles who gives orders inside the Hold's own walls, nothing more. It does not touch the attainder, does not require or wait on Tain's research, and carries no weight with the Concordat, the Compact, or anyone else outside the household. Phase 3 itself is unchanged and still gated on what Tain finds.
